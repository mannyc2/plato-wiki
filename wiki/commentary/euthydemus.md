They press Socrates from “I know something” to “I am knowing,” then forbid the words that would limit the claim—“whenever” and “that I know”—until “everything” and “always” can be attached to his answers. The maneuver exploits the answer procedure, but it also relies on the premise that the same thing cannot both be and not be at once. So the final claim is not a refutation by procedure alone; it is a conclusion produced by that procedure working on a contested premise.
