  At 967a-967d the Athenian reports that many suppose astronomy and the necessary arts connected with it make people atheistic because such students see events as produced by necessities rather than by deliberative purposes concerning goods. He then says the present position is the opposite: earlier investigators wondered how soulless bodies could use such exact calculations, some dared to say intellect orders the heavens, but the same people erred by making soul younger than bodies, with the result that the visible heavenly things appeared full of stone, earth, and other soulless bodies distributing the causes of the cosmos. He identifies that earlier account as producing many atheisms, difficulties, and poetic abuse of philosophers.

  The Athenian denies that the decisive details of the nocturnal council can yet be legislated in writing. The institution must be constructed through teaching and much association, beginning with a list of suitable people by age, learning capacity, character, and habit; written schedules of what and when to learn are called useless before knowledge of the timely moment arises in the learner's soul.

  At 968c-968e the Athenian says it is no longer possible to legislate laws about such matters before the council has been ordered; only then will the members legislate authoritatively about what they must govern. He says first a list must be drawn of those suited to the guard by age, powers for learning, character, and habits. He adds that what must be learned is hard to discover and hard to learn from another, and that writing down the times and occasions for each item is vain, since the learners themselves would not know that it is being learned at the right moment until knowledge has come to be inside each soul.

  This records the stated non-written and association-based formation process. It does not infer a general doctrine about all writing.
