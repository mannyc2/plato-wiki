observation: >-
  The political art is pressed with the same demand made of other arts: it must say what single target it looks to. The example chosen is virtue, which had been divided into four kinds but is also named as one thing, so the interlocutors must explain both the distinct names and the common name.
textual_basis: >-
  At 963b-963e the Athenian turns from pilot, medical, and generalship examples to the political case and asks what the one object is that political intellect looks toward. When Clinias and Megillus cannot articulate it, he says they must try to see it, beginning from the four kinds of virtue. Each of the four must be called one, yet all together are called by one name, virtue; the difference that gives courage and prudence different names is easier than saying how both, and the others, share the common name virtue.
limits: >-
