## What’s happening {#happening}

Crito agrees, and Socrates asks the next question: should a man keep an agreement that is just?[^agreed] Crito says yes. Then would leaving without the city’s consent harm those they should least harm, and break a just agreement?[^harm] Crito cannot answer; he does not understand.[^understand] So Socrates imagines the Laws and the state of Athens coming to him just as he is about to run away,[^laws] to ask whether he means to destroy them and the whole city.[^destroy] Could a city survive if anyone could set aside its courts’ verdicts?[^courts] Socrates asks Crito whether the two of them should answer that the city wronged him with an unjust verdict.[^unjust] Crito seizes on it.[^zeus] But the Laws ask whether he agreed to abide by the city’s verdicts.[^verdicts]

[^agreed]: crito 49e "ought a man to do what he has agreed to do, provided it is right, or may he violate his agreements?"
[^harm]: crito 50a "we are doing harm to the very ones to whom we least ought to do harm"
[^understand]: crito 50a "I cannot answer your question, Socrates, for I do not understand."
[^laws]: crito 50a "the laws and the commonwealth should come to me and ask"
[^destroy]: crito 50a "Are you not intending by this thing you are trying to do, to destroy us"
[^courts]: crito 50b "the decisions reached by the courts have no force but are made invalid and annulled by private persons"
[^unjust]: crito 50c "The state wronged me and did not judge the case rightly?"
[^zeus]: crito 50c "That is what we shall say, by Zeus, Socrates."
[^verdicts]: crito 50c "did you agree to abide by the verdicts pronounced by the state?"
