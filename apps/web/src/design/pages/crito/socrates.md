# Socrates

A condemned man with days to live, who wakes before dawn to find his friend Crito at his side,[^dawn] come with a plan to save him.[^save] He spends the morning showing why he will stay.

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="crito 43b" from="I have been wondering" to="sweetly you sleep" lit="how sweetly you sleep"}
Crito has sat by the bed in silence rather than wake him.
:::

:::passage{ref="crito 44a-b" from="I dreamed that" to="fertile Phthia"}
A dream, he believes, tells him the day he will die.
:::

## How he argues {#moves}

:::moves
1. He will follow only the argument that seems best on reflection, now as always.[^reason] {idea=truth-priority}
2. An athlete heeds his trainer, not the crowd. So whose opinion counts about right and wrong?[^trainer] {idea=expert-craft}
3. Not living, but living well, is what matters most.[^well]
4. One must never do wrong, not even to repay a wrong.[^repay]
5. He gives the Laws of Athens a voice and lets them question him.[^laws]
6. Their words drown out every other, as the flutes do for Cybele’s worshippers.[^flutes]
:::
