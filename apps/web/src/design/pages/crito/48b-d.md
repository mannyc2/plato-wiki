## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates allows that someone might object: the many have the power to put them to death.[^death] The argument stands all the same, and Crito agrees: what matters most is not living, but living well,[^well] and living well is living rightly.[^rightly] So the one question is whether it would be right for Socrates to escape without the Athenians’ consent.[^question] Crito’s points about money, reputation and the children, Socrates says, are the concerns of the crowd, who put men to death without a thought and would bring them back to life if they could.[^lightly] If escaping is wrong, then not even death should be weighed against it.[^only]

[^death]: crito 48b "it might, of course, be said that the multitude can put us to death"
[^well]: crito 48b "it is not living, but living well which we ought to consider most important"
[^rightly]: crito 48b "living well and living rightly are the same thing"
[^question]: crito 48b-d "the question is whether it is right for me to try to escape from here without the permission of the Athenians"
[^lightly]: crito 48c "the reflections of those who lightly put men to death, and would bring them to life again, if they could"
[^only]: crito 48d "but only the question of doing wrong"
