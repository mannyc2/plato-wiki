## What’s happening {#happening}

Crito has sat by him in silence, sleepless and sorrowful,[^sleepless] wondering at how sweetly he sleeps and how calmly he bears his fate.[^calm] So the dialogue opens on a contrast before any argument: the friend’s grief beside Socrates’ calm. When Crito points out that other men as old are upset all the same, Socrates grants it,[^true] and asks why he has come so early. He brings sad news, though apparently not sad to Socrates.[^news]

## Two views {#views}

:::views
- socrates: At his age, it would be absurd to be upset that he must die now.[^age]
- crito: Other men as old meet the same misfortune, and their age does not keep them from being upset.[^others]
:::

[^sleepless]: crito 43b "I only wish I myself were not so sleepless and sorrowful"
[^calm]: crito 43b "since you bear it so easily and calmly"
[^true]: crito 43c "That is true. But why have you come so early?"
[^news]: crito 43c "sad news, though apparently not sad to you"
[^age]: crito 43b-c "it would be absurd if at my age I were disturbed because I must die now"
[^others]: crito 43c "their age does not in the least prevent them from being disturbed by their fate"
