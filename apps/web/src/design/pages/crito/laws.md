# The Laws

The laws of Athens, as Socrates imagines them coming to him just as he is about to run away, to ask what he means to do.[^come] He speaks for them, and they have the last word: after them, Crito has nothing to say.[^nothing]

## Where we find them {#scene}

:::passage{ref="crito 50a" from="If, as I was on the point" to="what have you in mind to do?" lit="the laws and the commonwealth should come to me"}
Socrates brings them in when Crito cannot follow the argument.
:::

## How they argue {#moves}

:::moves
1. A city cannot stand if anyone may set aside its courts’ verdicts.[^courts]
2. They brought him into the world and saw to his upbringing, so he is their offspring.[^forth]
3. His country deserves more reverence than his parents: he must persuade her or obey.[^persuade]
4. Anyone may leave Athens, and whoever stays has agreed to obey.[^stays]
5. He agreed more firmly than most:[^most] he had seventy years to leave and stayed,[^seventy] and at his trial he preferred death to exile.[^exile]
6. Escape would endanger his friends,[^friends] shame him wherever he went,[^shame] and do nothing for his sons.[^sons]
7. If he dies now, he dies wronged by men, not by the laws.[^men]
:::
