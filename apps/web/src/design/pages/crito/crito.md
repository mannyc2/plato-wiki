# Crito

Socrates’ friend, an old man like him,[^old] with money to spend[^money] and a plan to get him out of prison that night.[^tonight] He brings every reason he can find, and by the end he has nothing left to say.[^nothing]

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="crito 43b" from="I only wish I myself" to="sweetly you sleep" lit="sleepless and sorrowful"}
Sleepless and grieving, he has sat for some time beside his sleeping friend.
:::

:::passage{ref="crito 44b" from="Too clear, apparently." to="never find again"}
He begs before the argument begins.
:::

## How he argues {#moves}

:::moves
1. He will lose a friend he can never replace, and people will say he would not spend money to save him.[^replace]
2. The crowd can do great harm to a man with a bad name.[^crowd]
3. The money is ready, his own and that of friends like Simmias and Cebes.[^simmias]
4. Friends in Thessaly will protect Socrates there.[^thessaly]
5. Staying is wrong: it betrays Socrates himself,[^betray] and his children.[^children]
6. It must all be done tonight.[^tonight]
:::
