## What’s happening {#happening}

Crito’s first reasons are his own loss and what people will say: that he could have saved his friend with money and would not spend it.[^money] Socrates asks why they should care so much what most people think;[^most] the most reasonable people, whose opinion is worth more, will understand things as they are really done.[^reasonable] Then Crito guesses at what holds Socrates back: fear that informers would make his friends pay for the escape.[^informers]

## Two views {#views}

:::views{unresolved}
- crito: The crowd can do almost the greatest harm to a man with a bad name, so its opinion matters.[^crowd]
- socrates: The crowd can do neither the greatest harm nor the greatest good, since it cannot make anyone wise or foolish; it acts at random.[^neither]
:::

[^informers]: crito 44e "the informers will make trouble for us by saying that we stole you away"
[^crowd]: crito 44d "the public is able to accomplish not by any means the least, but almost the greatest of evils, if one has a bad reputation with it"
[^neither]: crito 44d "they are not able to make a man wise or foolish, but they do whatever occurs to them"
