## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates makes the case with an athlete, and Crito agrees at every step.

:::moves
1. An athlete heeds the one man who knows his business, his doctor or trainer, not the crowd.[^trainer]
2. If he follows the crowd instead, he ruins his body.[^body]
3. In right and wrong too, we should follow the one who knows, if there is one, not the many.[^one]
4. A life with a ruined body is not worth living.[^ruined]
5. Nor is a life with the part of us ruined that wrong injures and right improves,[^part] and that part is worth far more than the body.[^more]
6. So what counts is not what the many say, but what the one who knows says, and the truth itself.[^truth]
:::

So, Socrates tells Crito, his plea began in the wrong place: with what the crowd thinks about right and wrong.[^wrongly]

[^trainer]: crito 47b "to those of one man only who is a physician or a trainer"
[^body]: crito 47c "Evidently upon his body; for that is what it ruins."
[^one]: crito 47d "the opinion of the many or that of the one, if there is anyone who knows about them"
[^ruined]: crito 47e "Then is life worth living when the body is worthless and ruined?"
[^part]: crito 47e "is it worth living when that is ruined which is injured by the wrong and improved by the right?"
[^more]: crito 48a "Much more."
[^truth]: crito 48a "what he who knows about right and wrong, the one man, and truth herself will say"
[^wrongly]: crito 48a "you introduced the discussion wrongly in the first place"
