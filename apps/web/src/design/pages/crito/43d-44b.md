## What’s happening {#happening}

The news is the ship from Delos, whose return sets the day Socrates must die.[^ship] Men who left it at Sunium say it will arrive today,[^sunium] so tomorrow his life must end.[^end] Socrates thinks it will come a day later,[^later] because of a dream he had that night. A beautiful woman dressed in white called to him[^woman] and said that on the third day he would come to fertile Phthia.[^phthia] Her words adapt a line of Homer’s Achilles, who says that sailing home he would reach fertile Phthia on the third day. Socrates takes them to name the day he will die: counting today, the day after tomorrow. Crito calls the dream strange; Socrates calls it clear.[^clear] Too clear, says Crito, and begs him, even now, to save himself.[^save]
