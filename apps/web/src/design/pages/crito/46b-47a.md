## What’s happening {#happening}

Crito’s eagerness is worth a great deal, Socrates says, if it is rightly directed; if not, the greater it is, the harder to bear.[^eager] But he has always followed only the argument that seems best to him on reflection,[^reason] and being condemned is no reason to throw out the arguments he has always made.[^same] Unless they can find better ones, he will not give way,[^better] however the crowd tries to frighten them both, as children are frightened with goblins.[^goblins] So they test an old agreement: that some opinions deserve respect and others do not,[^some] and that the good ones are the opinions of the wise.[^wise]

[^eager]: crito 46b "your eagerness is worth a great deal, if it should prove to be rightly directed; but otherwise, the greater it is, the more hard to bear"
[^reason]: crito 46b "follows nothing but the reasoning which on consideration seems to me best"
[^same]: crito 46b "discard the arguments I used to advance"
[^better]: crito 46c "unless we can bring forward better ones in our present situation, be assured that I shall not give way to you"
[^goblins]: crito 46c "as children are frightened with goblins"
[^some]: crito 47a "we ought not to esteem all the opinions of men, but some and not others"
[^wise]: crito 47a "the good ones are those of the wise and the bad ones those of the foolish"
