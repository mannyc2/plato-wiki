## What’s happening {#happening}

The Laws go on: any Athenian who dislikes them may take his goods and go wherever he likes.[^leave] Whoever stays has agreed to do what they command,[^stays] or else to persuade them they are wrong.[^either] Whoever does neither wrongs them three times over: as his parents, as those who raised him, and as the other party to his agreement.[^threefold] Socrates, they say, agreed more firmly than most.[^most] He hardly ever left the city, except on military service,[^festival] and he had his children there.[^children] At his trial he could have proposed exile, and said instead that he preferred death.[^exile] So he agreed not in words but by what he did,[^acts] and when Socrates asks, Crito grants it.[^must]

[^leave]: crito 51d "may take his goods and go away wherever he likes"
[^stays]: crito 51e "has thereby entered into an agreement with us to do what we command"
[^either]: crito 52a "either to convince us of error or to do our bidding"
[^threefold]: crito 51e "he who does not obey does threefold wrong"
[^most]: crito 52a "more emphatically than most other Athenians"
[^festival]: crito 52b "you never went out from the city to a festival, or anywhere else, except on military service"
[^children]: crito 52c "you begat children in the city, showing that it pleased you"
[^exile]: crito 52c "you preferred, as you said, death to exile"
[^acts]: crito 52d "you agreed, not in word, but by your acts, to live in accordance with us"
[^must]: crito 52d "We must, Socrates."
