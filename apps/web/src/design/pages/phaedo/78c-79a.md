## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates builds the answer in steps, and Cebes agrees to each.

:::moves
1. What is composite is naturally liable to come apart in the way it was put together,[^decomposed] and what is uncompounded, if anything is, is unlikely to.[^uncompounded]
2. Most probably, the things that are always the same are the uncompounded ones, and the things that keep changing are composite.[^probable]
3. Equality itself, beauty itself and every such essence is uniform and exists by itself, and never admits any change at all.[^change]
4. The many things that share their names, such as men, horses or cloaks called beautiful or equal, are never the same.[^never]
5. Those many things can be seen and touched; the things that stay the same can be grasped only by reason, and are invisible.[^reason]
6. So they agree to assume two kinds of existence: the invisible, always the same, and the visible, always changing.[^two]
:::

[^decomposed]: phaedo 78c "naturally liable to be decomposed, in the same way in which it was compounded"
[^uncompounded]: phaedo 78c "if anything is uncompounded is not that, if anything, naturally unlikely to be decomposed?"
[^probable]: phaedo 78c "things which are always the same and unchanging are the uncompounded things"
[^change]: phaedo 78d "since it is uniform and exists by itself, remain the same and never in any way admit of any change"
[^never]: phaedo 78e "they are never the same"
[^reason]: phaedo 79a "can be grasped only by the reason, and are invisible and not to be seen"
[^two]: phaedo 79a "the invisible is always the same and the visible constantly changing"
