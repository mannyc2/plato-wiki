## What’s happening {#happening}

Philosophy urges the soul, Socrates says, to trust nothing but itself and its own thought of what exists by itself,[^trust] and so the true philosopher’s soul stands aloof from pleasures, desires and griefs, so far as it can.[^aloof] For whoever feels these violently, Socrates says, suffers the greatest and most extreme evil, and does not take it into account.[^greatest] Asked by Cebes what that evil is, Socrates says that a soul greatly pleased or pained by something is compelled to believe that what caused it is very distinct and very true, though it is not.[^true] Each pleasure or pain, he says, nails the soul to the body as with a nail and makes it bodily,[^nail] so that it believes true whatever the body says is true.[^fancies] Such a soul can never depart in purity to the other world,[^purity] but soon sinks into another body and grows into it.[^sinks]

[^aloof]: phaedo 83b "it stands aloof from pleasures and lusts and griefs"
[^greatest]: phaedo 83c "he suffers the greatest and most extreme evil and does not take it into account"
[^true]: phaedo 83c "is compelled to believe that the object which caused the emotion is very distinct and very true; but it is not"
[^nail]: phaedo 83d "each pleasure or pain nails it as with a nail to the body and rivets it on and makes it corporeal"
[^fancies]: phaedo 83d "it fancies the things are true which the body says are true"
[^purity]: phaedo 83d "can never depart in purity to the other world"
