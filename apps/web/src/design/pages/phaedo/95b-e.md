## What’s happening {#happening}

Since Simmias’ argument could not withstand the first attack,[^withstand] Cebes would not be surprised if the argument of Cadmus met the same fate.[^cadmus] Socrates warns him not to be boastful, lest some evil eye put the coming argument to rout,[^boast] and restates what Cebes demands: a proof that the soul is indestructible and immortal,[^proof] if the philosopher who is confident in the face of death is not to find his confidence senseless and foolish.[^senseless] Showing that the soul is strong and godlike and existed before birth, as Socrates puts Cebes’ case, may bear witness only that it lasts a long while,[^while] and on that view its very entrance into the body was the beginning of its dissolution, a disease, as it were.[^disease] So, in Cebes’ view, anyone who does not know and cannot prove that the soul is immortal must fear, unless he is a fool.[^fear] Cebes has nothing to add or take away.[^add] Socrates pauses for some time, absorbed in thought,[^pause] then says that what Cebes seeks is no small thing: the cause of generation and decay must be completely investigated.[^cause]
