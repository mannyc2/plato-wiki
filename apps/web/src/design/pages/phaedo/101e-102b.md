## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates goes on: Cebes would keep assuming higher principles until he reached one that was adequate,[^adequate] and would not mix up the beginning and its consequences, as disputants do, if he wished to discover any of the realities.[^disputants] A philosopher, Socrates thinks, will do as he has said,[^philosopher] and Simmias and Cebes agree together.[^together] Here the story Phaedo is telling breaks off, and Echecrates and Phaedo speak in their own turns. By Zeus, Echecrates says, they were right: Socrates made those matters astonishingly clear to anyone with even a little sense.[^clear] Phaedo says all who were there thought so too,[^there] and Echecrates says so do he and those with him, who were not there and are hearing it now, and asks what was said next.[^next] As Phaedo remembers it,[^remember] once they had agreed that each of these qualities exists and that other things which participate in them take their names from them,[^names] Socrates asked whether, when they say Simmias is greater than Socrates and smaller than Phaedo, there is greatness and smallness in Simmias.[^simmias] Yet, Socrates said, the statement that Simmias is greater than Socrates is not true as those words put it.[^words]
