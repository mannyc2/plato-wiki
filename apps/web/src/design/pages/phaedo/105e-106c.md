## What’s happening {#happening}

What does not admit death, Cebes answers, is deathless or immortal;[^deathless] he agrees that the soul does not admit death, and so is immortal.[^immortal] He thinks this proved, and very satisfactorily.[^proved] Socrates goes on: if the odd were necessarily imperishable, the number three would be imperishable too,[^three] and if what is without heat were imperishable, snow would go away whole and unmelted whenever heat came against it.[^snow] So, he says, if the immortal is also imperishable, it is impossible for the soul to perish when death comes against it.[^perish] But someone might ask, Socrates says, why the odd should not perish when the even comes against it, and the even take its place.[^objection] Socrates grants that this cannot be silenced by saying the odd does not perish, for the odd is not imperishable.[^silence] If it is conceded that the immortal is imperishable, he says, the soul would be imperishable as well as immortal.[^conceded]
