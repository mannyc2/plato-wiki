## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates asks whether they are not made up of two parts, body and soul,[^parts] and Cebes says the body is clearly more like the visible.[^visible] The soul is invisible, Cebes says, to human beings at least;[^man] but since they call things visible and invisible with reference to human sight,[^human] Socrates concludes that the soul is more like the invisible than the body is.[^more] He recalls what they said before: when the soul uses the body to inquire through the senses, it is dragged to things that never stay the same, and wanders, confused and dizzy like a drunken man.[^drunk] When it inquires alone by itself, it goes to the pure, everlasting, immortal and changeless, and being akin to them it stays with them;[^akin] that state of the soul, Socrates says, is called wisdom.[^wisdom] Cebes says anyone, even the dullest, would agree that the soul is infinitely more like what is always the same,[^dullest] and the body more like the other.[^other]
