# Phaedo

A friend who was with Socrates on the day he drank the poison,[^there] and who tells the whole story to Echecrates.[^beginning] Being reminded of Socrates, he says, is always his greatest pleasure.[^pleasure]

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="phaedo 59a" from="And for this reason I was not at all filled with pity" to="presently to die." lit="an unaccustomed mixture of pleasure and of pain together"}
Not pity, he says, but pleasure and pain mixed together.
:::

:::passage{ref="phaedo 89a-b" from="I was sitting at his right hand" to="cut off this beautiful hair." lit="this beautiful hair"}
Here Socrates, sitting above him, strokes his hair and turns to him.
:::

## What he does {#moves}

:::moves
1. He explains why Socrates died long after his trial. The mission to Delos had begun the day before it,[^crowned] and by Athenian law, once the mission begins, no one may be publicly executed until the ship has gone to Delos and back.[^ship]
2. He names the friends who were there, and thinks Plato was ill.[^plato]
3. After the two objections, he tells Echecrates, they were all made to fear that their judgment was worthless or that no certainty could be had in these matters.[^worthless]
4. Asked whether Socrates showed any uneasiness, he says he never admired him more than then.[^admire]
5. When Socrates says that in his place he would swear, like the Argives, not to let his hair grow until he had beaten the argument of Simmias and Cebes,[^argives] Phaedo answers that, as they say, even Heracles is no match for two,[^heracles] and calls on Socrates to help, as Iolaus calling Heracles.[^iolaus]
6. When Socrates warns him against becoming a hater of argument,[^misology] Phaedo agrees that such a man’s case would be sad.[^sad]
7. He does not clearly remember who it was that later objected that opposites had earlier been agreed to come from opposites.[^forgot]
:::

## Also in the Phaedo {#cast}

:::people
- socrates: The friend he lost; when Socrates drank, Phaedo wept, he says, not for Socrates but for his own misfortune.[^wept]
- echecrates: The listener he tells it to, who asks him to tell everything as exactly as he can.[^exactly]
- crito: Could not restrain his tears, even before Phaedo broke down,[^crito] yet was still there at the end to close Socrates’ mouth and eyes.[^closed]
:::
