## What’s happening {#happening}

Phaedo thought Socrates was pleased by Cebes’ earnestness,[^pleased] and Socrates says Cebes is always on the track of arguments and not easily convinced.[^track] This time Simmias thinks Cebes is right,[^right] and that Cebes is aiming his argument at Socrates, who is so ready to leave his friends and the gods, who are, as Socrates himself agrees, good rulers.[^aiming] Socrates gathers that he must defend himself against this accusation as if in a law court,[^court] and he will try to make a more convincing defence than he made before the judges.[^defence] If Socrates did not believe he was going to other wise and good gods, and to men who have died, better than those here,[^gods] he says he would be wrong not to grieve at death.[^grieve] He expects to go to good men, though he would not care to assert this positively;[^men] that he is going to gods who are good masters, he would assert as positively as anything about such matters.[^masters] So he is hopeful that something is in store for the dead,[^hopes] and, as has been said of old, something better for the good than for the wicked.[^old]
