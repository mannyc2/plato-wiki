## What’s happening {#happening}

Cebes agrees[^certainly] that the number three will endure destruction or anything else rather than become even while still remaining three,[^three] though two is not the opposite of three.[^two] So certain things besides opposite ideas refuse to admit the approach of opposites,[^refuse] and Socrates proposes to determine what these are.[^determine] He suggests they are the things that compel whatever they take possession of to take not only their own form but that of some opposite:[^compel] whatever the number three is an essential element of must be not only three but also odd.[^odd] Such a thing, they agree, will never admit the even, so three has no part in the even[^part] and is uneven.[^uneven] Socrates puts the general statement: not only will opposites not admit their opposites, but nothing that brings an opposite to whatever it approaches will ever admit the opposite of what it brings.[^statement] Five, he says, will not admit the idea of the even,[^five] nor will ten, the double of five, admit the idea of the odd.[^ten]
