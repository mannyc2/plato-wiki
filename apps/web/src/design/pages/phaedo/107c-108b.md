## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates turns to his friends: if the soul is immortal, they must care for it not only for this time we call life but for all time,[^care] and if they neglect it, the danger now appears terrible.[^danger] If death were an escape from everything, it would be a boon to the wicked;[^boon] but since the soul is seen to be immortal, it can be saved only by becoming as good and wise as possible.[^wise] The soul, he says, takes nothing with it to the other world but its education and nurture.[^nurture] It is said, he goes on, that after death each person’s tutelary genius leads him to a place where the dead are gathered together,[^genius] and there they are judged.[^judged] Telephus in Aeschylus’ play says a simple path leads to the lower world,[^telephus] but Socrates thinks the path is neither simple nor single,[^path] for there seem to be many forks of the road and many windings, which he infers from the rites and ceremonies practiced on earth.[^rites] In his account, the orderly and wise soul follows its guide,[^orderly] but the soul desirous of the body is led away with violence after much resistance,[^violence] and a soul that has done wrong, by wicked murders or the like, is shunned by all, and no one is willing to be its companion or guide.[^shunned]
