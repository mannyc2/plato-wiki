## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates sat up on his couch, bent his leg and rubbed it.[^rub] How strange a thing, he says, seems to be what men call pleasure,[^strange] and how wonderfully it is related to pain, which seems to be its opposite.[^related] The two will not both come to a man at the same time,[^same] yet whoever pursues and captures the one is generally obliged to take the other too.[^both] If Aesop had thought of them, Socrates says, he would have made a fable,[^aesop] in which god, unable to reconcile them, fastened their heads together.[^heads] So in his own case, after the pain the fetter caused in his leg, pleasure appears to have come following after.[^leg] Here Cebes breaks in:[^interrupted] people have been asking about the verses Socrates has composed, his metrical versions of Aesop’s fables and the hymn to Apollo,[^poems] and Evenus has asked Cebes why Socrates, who never wrote any poetry before, composed these after he came to prison.[^evenus] Socrates tells Cebes to give Evenus the truth: Socrates did not compose them because he wished to rival Evenus or his poems.[^rival]
