## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias sets out his objection in steps.

:::moves
1. In a well attuned lyre the harmony is divine, but the lyre and its strings are bodies, composite, earthy and akin to the mortal.[^lyre]
2. If someone broke the lyre, a person using Socrates’ argument might maintain that the harmony must still exist somewhere, and that the wood and strings must rot away before anything could happen to it.[^still]
3. Simmias fancies it has occurred to Socrates too that they believe the soul is something like this: when the heat, cold, moisture and dryness that hold the body together are well and properly mixed, the soul is their mixture and harmony.[^mixture]
4. If so, when disease or other ills make the body too slack or too tight, the soul must perish at once, however divine, like other harmonies in sounds and in works of art,[^perish] while the body’s remains last a long time.[^remains]
5. What, then, can they say if someone claims that the soul, being a mixture of the body’s elements, is the first to perish in what is called death?[^first]
:::

Phaedo recalls that Socrates looked keenly at them, as he often used to do, and smiled,[^smiled] and said that Simmias raised a fair objection.[^fair]

[^lyre]: phaedo 86a "divine in the well attuned lyre, but the lyre itself and its strings are bodies"
[^still]: phaedo 86b "He would say that the harmony must still exist somewhere, and that the wood and the strings must rot away before anything could happen to it."
[^mixture]: phaedo 86b-c "held together by heat, cold, moisture, dryness, and the like, and the soul is a mixture and a harmony of these same elements, when they are well and properly mixed"
[^perish]: phaedo 86c "the soul must of necessity perish, no matter how divine it is, like other harmonies in sounds and in all the works of artists"
[^remains]: phaedo 86c-d "the remains of each body will endure a long time"
[^first]: phaedo 86d "if anyone claims that the soul, being a mixture of the elements of the body, is the first to perish in what is called death"
[^smiled]: phaedo 86d "looking keenly at us, as he often used to do, smiled"
[^fair]: phaedo 86d "Simmias raises a fair objection"
