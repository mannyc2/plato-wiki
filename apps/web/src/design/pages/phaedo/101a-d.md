## What’s happening {#happening}

Cebes, Socrates says, would insist that every greater thing is greater by nothing else than greatness, not by a head.[^greatness] He would be afraid of the retort that the greater man is then greater by a head, which is small, and that it is a monstrous thing to be great by something small.[^monstrous] Cebes laughs and agrees he would.[^laughed] For the same reason, Socrates goes on, Cebes would say ten is more than eight by number, not by two,[^number] and Cebes agrees.[^certainly] Cebes would also avoid saying that adding one to one, or dividing one, is the cause of two,[^avoid] and accept no other cause of two than participation in duality,[^duality] just as whatever is to be one must participate in unity.[^unity] Clinging to that safe principle,[^safe] he would not reply to anyone who attacked it until he had examined whether its consequences agreed with one another.[^consequences] And to give an account of the principle itself, he would assume another, the one that seemed best of the higher ones, and so on.[^higher]

[^monstrous]: phaedo 101b "the greater man is greater by a head, which is small, and that it is a monstrous thing that one is great by something that is small"
[^laughed]: phaedo 101b "Cebes laughed and said, Yes, I should."
[^number]: phaedo 101b "You would say it is more by number and by reason of number"
[^certainly]: phaedo 101b "Certainly, said he."
[^avoid]: phaedo 101c "you would avoid saying that the addition or the division is the cause of two"
[^duality]: phaedo 101c "you accept no other cause of the existence of two than participation in duality"
[^unity]: phaedo 101c "whatever is to be one must participate in unity"
[^safe]: phaedo 101d "so you would cling to that safe principle of ours"
