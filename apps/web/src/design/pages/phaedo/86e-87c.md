## What’s happening {#happening}

Before replying, Socrates says, they ought to hear what fault Cebes finds with the argument, so as to have time to consider what to say.[^fault] Cebes grants that it has been very cleverly and, if he may say so, conclusively shown that the soul existed before it entered this bodily form,[^shown] but not that it will still exist when they are dead.[^dead] He does not side with Simmias: he thinks the soul far superior to the body in strength and endurance.[^superior] Why then, the argument might ask him, does he still disbelieve, when he sees that after a man dies the weaker part still exists,[^weaker] and must not the stronger part be preserved at least as long?[^stronger] Like Simmias, Cebes answers with a figure.[^figure] It is, he says, as if someone claimed that an old weaver who had died was safe somewhere, offering as proof that the cloak he had woven and used to wear was still whole,[^cloak] and then reasoned that since a man lasts much longer than a cloak, the man must surely be safe.[^lasts] Anyone can understand, Cebes tells Simmias, that such a man is talking nonsense.[^nonsense]
