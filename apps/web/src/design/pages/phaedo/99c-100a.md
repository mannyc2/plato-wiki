## What’s happening {#happening}

Those who explain the earth in such ways, Socrates says, do not look for the power that places things as it is best for them to be,[^power] and give no thought to the good, which must embrace and hold together all things.[^good] He would gladly be the pupil of anyone who could teach him such a cause,[^pupil] but that was denied him, and he could not discover it himself.[^denied] So he offers Cebes an account of his second voyage in quest of the cause,[^voyage] and Cebes wishes it with all his heart.[^heart] Having given up investigating realities, he says, he decided he must be careful not to suffer what happens to people who watch the sun during an eclipse,[^gaveup] some of whom ruin their eyes unless they look at its image in water,[^eclipse] and Socrates was afraid his soul would be blinded in the same way if he tried to grasp things with his senses.[^blinded] So he had recourse to conceptions and examined in them the truth of realities,[^conceptions] though he does not grant that studying realities through conceptions is looking at images any more than studying them in the facts of daily life.[^images] In each case he assumes the principle he considers strongest,[^principle] and regards as true whatever seems to agree with it, and as untrue whatever disagrees.[^agree] Cebes does not yet understand very well.[^well]
