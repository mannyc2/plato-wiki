## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias says the argument reaches the conclusion that their soul existed before birth, just as the essence Socrates speaks of exists,[^essence] and nothing is so clear to him as that the beautiful, the good and the rest have a most real existence.[^real] Cebes is convinced too, Simmias thinks, though he is the most obstinately incredulous of mortals.[^obstinate] But that the soul will still exist after death does not seem proved even to Simmias;[^proved] the common fear remains that when a man dies the soul is dispersed and that is the end of it.[^dispersed] Cebes agrees that only half of what is required has been proved.[^half] Socrates answers that it has been shown already, if they combine this argument with the earlier one, that every living being is born from the dead:[^combine] if the soul exists before birth and can be born only from death, must it not also exist after dying, since it must be born again?[^again] Still, he thinks Cebes and Simmias would like to go on, and that they have the childish fear that the wind will really blow the soul away as it leaves the body.[^wind]
