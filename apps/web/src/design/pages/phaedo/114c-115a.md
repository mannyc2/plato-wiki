## What’s happening {#happening}

In Socrates’ story, the holy mount upward to their pure abode and dwell upon the earth,[^abode] and those who have duly purified themselves by philosophy live henceforth altogether without bodies, in abodes still more beautiful.[^philosophy] Because of all this, he tells Simmias, they ought to do their best to acquire virtue and wisdom in life, for the prize is fair and the hope great.[^prize] Socrates says it would not be fitting for a man of sense to maintain that all this is just as he has described it.[^sense] But since the soul is shown to be immortal, he thinks a man may properly venture to believe that this or something like it is true of our souls and their abodes,[^venture] for the venture is well worth while.[^worth] A man ought to repeat such things to himself like magic charms, Socrates says, which is why he has lengthened out the story.[^charms] So, Socrates concludes, a man should be of good cheer about his soul[^cheer] if he has rejected the pleasures and ornaments of the body[^pleasures] and adorned his soul with its own proper adornment of self-restraint and justice and courage and freedom and truth.[^adornment] The others will go each in his own time, Socrates says,[^time] but fate, as a tragedian would say, calls him now, and it is time for his bath,[^bath] so that the women need not bathe the corpse.[^corpse]
