## What’s happening {#happening}

The speech Socrates gives the lovers of wisdom ends: it cannot be that the impure attain the pure.[^impure] Such words, Socrates thinks, all who are rightly lovers of knowledge must say to each other,[^words] and Simmias agrees most assuredly.[^assuredly] If this is true, Socrates has great hopes that where he is going he will, there if anywhere, attain fully what has been his chief object in life,[^hopes] and the like hope exists for every man who thinks his mind has been purified and made ready.[^ready] Purification, Socrates suggests and Simmias grants, consists in separating the soul from the body so far as possible,[^separating] teaching it to collect itself together from all parts of the body,[^collect] and to live, so far as it can, both now and hereafter, alone by itself, freed from the body as from fetters.[^fetters] Simmias agrees that this release and separation from the body is what they call death.[^release] As he and Simmias hold, Socrates says, the true philosophers and they alone are always most eager to release the soul,[^eager] and this release and separation is their study.[^study]
