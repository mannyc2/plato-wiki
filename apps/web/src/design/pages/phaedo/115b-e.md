## What’s happening {#happening}

Crito asks whether Socrates has any directions about his children or anything else they can do for him.[^directions] Nothing new, Socrates replies: if they take care of themselves, they will serve him and his and themselves, whatever they do,[^care] but if they will not follow the path their discussions have marked out, they will accomplish nothing, however much they promise now.[^nothing] Crito says they will try, and asks how they should bury him.[^bury] As they please, Socrates answers, if they can catch him and he does not get away from them.[^catch] Then he laughs gently and tells the others he cannot persuade Crito that the Socrates now conversing is really himself;[^conversing] Crito, he says, thinks he is the one Crito will presently see as a corpse.[^corpse] So Socrates asks them to give Crito the opposite of the security Crito gave the judges at Socrates’ trial: security that Socrates will not remain when he dies, but will go away.[^security] Crito should not say at the funeral that he is burying Socrates,[^funeral] but that he buries Socrates’ body,[^body] for such wrong words, Socrates says, are not only undesirable in themselves but infect the soul with evil.[^infect]
