## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates does not believe anyone who heard them, even a comic poet, would say he is chattering about things that do not concern him,[^comic] so he proposes they examine the matter to the end.[^end] He asks whether the souls of men who have died are in the nether world,[^nether] and starts from an ancient tradition that they go there from here, come back again, and are born from the dead.[^tradition] If the living are born again from the dead, he reasons, our souls would exist there,[^there] for they could not be born again if they did not exist.[^born] That would be a sufficient proof, he says, if it should really be made evident that the living are born only from the dead;[^sufficient] if not, some other argument would be needed.[^other] To find out easily, Socrates says, they should look not at men only but at all animals and plants,[^plants] and ask whether everything that has an opposite must be generated from its opposite and from it only.[^opposite] When anything becomes greater, for instance, it must inevitably have been smaller first, and Cebes agrees.[^greater]
