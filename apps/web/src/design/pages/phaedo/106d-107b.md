## What’s happening {#happening}

Cebes thinks no further argument is needed for that: nothing would escape destruction if the immortal, which is everlasting, were perishable.[^everlasting] Socrates thinks all would agree that God and the Principle of life, and anything else that is immortal, can never perish,[^god] and Cebes adds that all men would, and still more, he fancies, the gods.[^gods] Since the immortal is indestructible, then, the soul, if it is immortal, will be imperishable too, and Cebes says this follows necessarily.[^imperishable] So, they agree, when death comes to a man, his mortal part, it seems, dies, but the immortal part goes away unharmed.[^unharmed] Socrates calls it perfectly certain that the soul is immortal and imperishable, and that their souls will exist somewhere in another world.[^certain] Cebes has nothing more to say against it,[^nothing] and if Simmias or anyone else has something to say, he would do well to speak now.[^speak] Simmias cannot doubt the result either, but the subject is so great, and he has so poor an opinion of human weakness, that some doubt remains in his mind.[^doubt] Socrates does not dismiss the doubt: not only that, he says, but their first assumptions ought to be examined more carefully, even though they seem certain;[^assumptions] if they analyze them completely, he thinks, they will follow the argument as far as a man can.[^follow] Simmias agrees.[^true]
