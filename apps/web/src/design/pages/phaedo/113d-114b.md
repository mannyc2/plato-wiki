## What’s happening {#happening}

When the dead arrive, Socrates’ story goes on, they are first judged as they have lived well and piously, or not.[^judged] Those found to have lived neither well nor ill go to the Acheron and on to the lake,[^neither] where they are purified and absolved of any wrong by paying the penalty,[^purified] and receive rewards for their good deeds.[^rewards] Those who appear to be incurable, because of the greatness of their wrongdoings,[^incurable] such as many great deeds of sacrilege or wicked murders, are cast into Tartarus, whence they never emerge.[^never] Those who are curable but have committed great sins, such as violence against father or mother in a moment of passion,[^passion] followed by a life of repentance, or who have killed someone in a similar way,[^repentance] are thrown into Tartarus too, and after a year the wave casts them out.[^year] Brought to the Acherusian lake, they call to those they have slain or outraged, begging them to let them come out;[^begging] if they prevail, their ills end, and if not, they are borne back to Tartarus, until they do.[^prevail] Those found to have excelled in holy living are freed from these regions within the earth, as from prisons.[^prisons]
