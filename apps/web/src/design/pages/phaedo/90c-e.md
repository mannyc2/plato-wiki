## What’s happening {#happening}

Those who have spent their time in disputation, Socrates says, end by thinking themselves the wisest of men,[^wisest] who alone have discovered that nothing is sound or sure, but all things go up and down, like the tide in the Euripus.[^euripus] If there is an argument that is true and sure and can be grasped,[^system] it would be sad, he tells Phaedo, if a man who had met arguments that seemed sometimes true and sometimes false did not blame himself or his own lack of skill,[^skill] but gladly threw the blame on arguments and hated and reviled them for the rest of his life, deprived of the truth and knowledge of reality.[^deprived] Phaedo says that by Zeus it would be sad.[^zeus] So, Socrates says, they must not let into their souls the notion that there is no soundness in arguments at all,[^soundness] but rather assume that they themselves are not yet in sound condition and must strive manfully to become so,[^strive] Phaedo and the others for the sake of all their future life.[^future]
