## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates prays to the gods that his departure be a fortunate one,[^pray] and drains the cup very cheerfully and quietly.[^drained] Phaedo tells how his own tears then rolled down in floods, and he wrapped his face in his cloak,[^cloak] weeping not for Socrates but for his own misfortune in losing such a friend.[^friend] Crito had already got up, unable to restrain his tears,[^crito] and Apollodorus wailed aloud and made all of them break down, except Socrates.[^apollodorus] Socrates says he sent the women away chiefly for this reason, for he has heard that it is best to die in silence,[^silence] and they are ashamed and control their tears.[^ashamed] He walks about, and when he says his legs are heavy he lies down on his back, as the attendant advised;[^lies] the man pinches his foot hard and asks if he feels it, and Socrates says no.[^foot] Moving upward from the feet to the shins, the man shows them that Socrates is growing cold and rigid,[^cold] and says that when it reaches his heart, Socrates will be gone.[^heart] Uncovering his face, Socrates says his last words, to Crito: they owe a cock to Aesculapius, and he tells his friends to pay it and not neglect it.[^cock] Crito says it shall be done and asks if there is anything else, but Socrates makes no reply;[^reply] after a little while he moves, the attendant uncovers him, and his eyes are fixed; Crito closes his mouth and eyes.[^eyes] Phaedo ends by telling Echecrates that this was the end of their friend, who was, as they may say, of all those of his time whom they have known, the best and wisest and most righteous man.[^best]
