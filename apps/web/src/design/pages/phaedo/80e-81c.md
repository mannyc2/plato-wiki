## What’s happening {#happening}

Far from it, Socrates tells Cebes and Simmias: the soul is not scattered when it leaves the body.[^far] If the soul departs pure, dragging nothing of the body with it,[^pure] because in life it avoided the body and gathered itself into itself, that is nothing else, he says, than pursuing philosophy rightly and really practising being dead.[^practice] Such a soul goes away to what is like it, the invisible, divine, immortal and wise, and there it is happy, freed from error, folly, fear, fierce loves and the other human ills,[^happy] and, as is said of the initiated, it lives with the gods for all time after.[^gods] But suppose, Socrates says, a soul leaves the body defiled and impure,[^defiled] because it was always with the body and loved it, so that it thought nothing true except the bodily.[^corporeal] Cebes says such a soul will by no means depart pure.[^nomeans] Such a soul, Socrates supposes, is permeated with the bodily, which constant company with the body has made part of its nature;[^nature] and since the bodily is heavy and earthly, the soul is weighed down by it and dragged back into the visible world, through fear of the invisible.[^dragged]
