## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates goes into another room to bathe, with Crito following, and tells the rest to wait.[^bathe] They waited, Phaedo recalls, talking over the discussion and their great misfortune, for they felt he was like a father to them and that they would pass the rest of their lives as orphans.[^orphans] His children are brought to him, two little sons and one big one, and the women of the family come;[^children] he talks with them in Crito’s presence, then tells the women to go away and comes back to his friends.[^women] It is now nearly sunset.[^sunset] The servant of the Eleven comes and stands beside him.[^servant] Others, the servant says, are angry and curse him when he tells them, at the behest of the authorities, to drink the poison, but he will not find fault with Socrates as he does with them;[^angry] he has found Socrates the noblest and gentlest and best man who ever came there.[^noblest] He bids Socrates farewell, bursts into tears, and goes away.[^tears] Socrates bids him farewell too, and tells the others how charming the man is, and how nobly he weeps for him.[^nobly] Then Socrates asks Crito to have the poison brought, if it is ready.[^poison]
