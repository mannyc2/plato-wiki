## What’s happening {#happening}

Phaedo tells Echecrates that all of them were very uncomfortable at what Simmias and Cebes said,[^uncomfortable] for they had been thoroughly convinced, and now felt thrown back into confusion and distrust,[^confusion] afraid that their judgment was worthless or that no certainty could be reached in these matters.[^worthless] Here Phaedo’s story breaks off, and Echecrates speaks in his own turn: he feels for them,[^sympathize] since he too now asks himself what argument can be trusted from now on.[^believe] The doctrine that the soul is a kind of harmony, he says, has always had, and still has, a wonderful hold on him,[^hold] and he must find another argument to convince him that the soul does not perish with the man.[^another] He asks whether Socrates showed any uneasiness or calmly defended his argument.[^calmly] Phaedo answers in his own turn that he never admired Socrates more,[^admired] for the gentle and respectful way he listened to the young men’s criticisms,[^manner] his quick sense of what their words had done to the listeners,[^sense] and the skill with which he cured Phaedo and the others and rallied them.[^cured] Echecrates asks how Socrates did it, and Phaedo takes up the story again.[^how]
