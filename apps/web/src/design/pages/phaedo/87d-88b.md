## What’s happening {#happening}

Cebes draws out what his figure shows.

:::moves
1. The weaver outlasted many cloaks but perished, Cebes supposes, before the last one,[^last] and that does not make a man weaker than a cloak.[^weaker]
2. In the same way, one might say, the soul lasts long and the body is weaker, and each soul wears out many bodies, especially if the man lives many years.[^many]
3. If the body is always being destroyed while the man lives and the soul keeps weaving it anew,[^weaving] then when the soul perishes it must have on its last garment, and only that will survive it; after that the body quickly decays.[^garment]
4. One might grant even more: that nothing prevents some souls from going on existing after death, and being born and dying again many times.[^grant]
5. Even so, one might not grant that the soul does not suffer from its many births and finally perish altogether in one of its deaths,[^perish] and might say that no one knows beforehand which death that will be.[^knows]
6. If that is so, anyone who feels confident about death has a foolish confidence, unless he can show that the soul is altogether immortal and imperishable.[^foolish]
:::

Otherwise, Cebes says, a man about to die must always fear that his soul will perish utterly when his body is dissolved.[^fear]
