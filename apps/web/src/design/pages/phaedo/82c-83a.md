## What’s happening {#happening}

Only the lover of knowledge, Socrates says, may join the gods,[^only] and this, he tells Simmias and Cebes, is why those who truly love wisdom refrain from all bodily desires:[^refrain] not, like most men who love money, for fear of poverty or loss of property,[^poverty] nor, like the lovers of honour and power, for fear of dishonour or disgrace.[^disgrace] Cebes agrees that would not be seemly for them.[^seemly] Those who care for their own souls, Socrates says, believe that philosophy, with its deliverance and purification, must not be resisted, and follow it wherever it leads.[^resist] Lovers of knowledge see, he explains, that when philosophy takes hold of their soul, the soul is welded to the body and made to view realities through it as through prison bars;[^bars] philosophy sees that the worst of this imprisonment is that it comes about through desire,[^lusts] so that the prisoner himself is the chief assistant in his own imprisonment.[^chief] Philosophy encourages the soul gently and tries to set it free, Socrates says, showing it that the eyes, the ears and the other senses are full of deceit.[^deceit]
