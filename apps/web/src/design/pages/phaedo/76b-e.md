## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias cannot choose at the moment,[^cannot] so Socrates asks him something he can answer: can a man who knows give an account of what he knows?[^account] Simmias says certainly; but asked whether everyone can give an account of these matters, he fears that by this time tomorrow no man living will be able to do so properly.[^tomorrow] Then not all men know them, Socrates says, and they recollect what they once learned.[^recollect] Since they did not acquire that knowledge after being born as human beings, their souls existed before, apart from bodies, and had intelligence.[^apart] Simmias tries one way out: perhaps they acquire the knowledge at the moment of birth.[^moment] Socrates asks at what other time they could lose it, since they are not born with it, and Simmias admits he was talking nonsense.[^nonsense] If the beautiful and the good and every such essence exist, as they are always saying,[^exist] and if they refer everything from the senses to these, finding that these existed before and were already theirs,[^refer] then, Socrates argues, their souls existed before they were born; and if these things do not exist, the argument has no force.[^force] It seems to Simmias that the certainty is absolutely the same.[^same]
