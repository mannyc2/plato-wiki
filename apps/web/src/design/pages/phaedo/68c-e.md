## What’s happening {#happening}

Such a man, Socrates adds, is also a lover of money and of honor, one or both,[^money] and Simmias agrees.[^agree] What is called courage, Simmias grants, is especially characteristic of philosophers,[^courage] and so is what is commonly called self-restraint, not being excited by the passions but superior to them,[^passions] which belongs to those alone who despise the body and pass their lives in philosophy.[^alone] If Simmias cares to consider the courage and self-restraint of other men, Socrates says, he will see that they are absurd.[^absurd] All other men count death among the great evils,[^evils] and brave men face death, when they do face it, through fear of greater evils;[^greater] so all except philosophers are brave through fear, and it is absurd to be brave through fear and cowardice.[^cowardice] The self-restrained are in the same case, Socrates says: they are self-restrained because of a kind of self-indulgence.[^indulgence] Socrates grants that this is called impossible, yet their foolish self-restraint, he says, amounts to little more than this,[^little] since they fear being deprived of certain pleasures they desire,[^deprived] and so refrain from some pleasures because they are under the sway of others.[^sway]
