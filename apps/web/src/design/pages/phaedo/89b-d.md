## What’s happening {#happening}

Phaedo, now a speaker in his own story, was sitting beside Socrates’ couch, lower than him.[^stool] Socrates stroked Phaedo’s head and gathered the hair on the back of his neck,[^hair] and said that tomorrow, perhaps, Phaedo would cut off this beautiful hair.[^tomorrow] Not if he took his advice, Socrates said: they should both cut their hair today, if their argument died and they could not bring it back to life.[^today] If he were Phaedo, Socrates said, he would take an oath like the Argives not to let it grow until he had won against the argument of Simmias and Cebes.[^oath] When Phaedo answered that, as the saying goes, even Heracles is no match for two,[^two] Socrates told him to call on him as his Iolaus while there was still light,[^iolaus] and Phaedo did, not as Heracles calling Iolaus, but as Iolaus calling Heracles.[^calling] But first, Socrates said, they must guard against becoming misologists, haters of argument, as people become haters of men,[^misology] for no worse evil can happen to a man than to hate argument.[^worse] Misology and misanthropy, he explains, arise from similar causes:[^causes] misanthropy comes from trusting someone implicitly without sufficient knowledge,[^trusting] then finding him base and false, and the same again with another.[^false]
