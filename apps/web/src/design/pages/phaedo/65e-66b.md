## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates asks whether the true nature of such things is contemplated by means of the body,[^body] or whether the man who prepares most carefully to understand the true essence of each thing would come nearest to knowing it, and Simmias says certainly.[^nearest] That man would do it most perfectly, Socrates goes on, who approaches each thing, so far as possible, with the reason alone,[^reason] not dragging in sight or any of the other senses,[^senses] and who removes himself, so far as possible, from his whole body,[^remove] because he feels that its companionship disturbs the soul and hinders it from attaining truth and wisdom.[^companionship] Simmias calls this true as true can be.[^true] All this, Socrates says, must lead good lovers of wisdom to say to one another something like this:[^lovers] there seems to be a short cut leading to the conclusion that, so long as we have the body and the soul is contaminated by such an evil, we shall never completely attain what we desire, the truth.[^truth] For the body, as Socrates has them say, keeps us constantly busy by its need of sustenance.[^sustenance]
