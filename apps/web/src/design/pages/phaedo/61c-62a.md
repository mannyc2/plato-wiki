## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates adds that he is going today, it seems, by order of the Athenians,[^today] and Simmias, from what he has seen of Evenus, says he will not take that advice in the least if he can help it.[^simmias] Socrates asks whether Evenus is not a philosopher, and Simmias thinks so.[^thinks] Then, Socrates says, Evenus will take his advice, and so will every man with any worthy interest in philosophy;[^every] perhaps, though, he will not take his own life, for they say that is not permitted.[^permitted] As he spoke, Phaedo recalls, Socrates put his feet down on the ground and stayed sitting that way for the rest of the conversation.[^feet] Cebes asks what Socrates means: that it is not permitted to take one’s own life, and yet the philosopher would desire to follow after the dying.[^cebes] Socrates asks whether Cebes and Simmias, who spent time with Philolaus, have not heard about such things, and Cebes answers, nothing definite.[^nothing] Socrates himself speaks of them only from hearsay.[^hearsay] Cebes has heard Philolaus, and others too, say that one must not kill oneself,[^philolaus] but never heard anyone say anything definite about it.[^definite] Socrates answers that it will perhaps seem strange to Cebes that people for whom it is better to die cannot without impiety do good to themselves, but must wait for some other benefactor,[^benefactor] and Cebes, smiling gently, says in his own dialect that God knows it does.[^dialect]
