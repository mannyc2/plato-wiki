# Echecrates

Echecrates has heard little from Athens. None of the Phliasians go there now, he says,[^phliasians] and no one has come who could tell them anything definite about Socrates’ death beyond the bare fact that he drank poison and died.[^died] He hears the story from Phaedo, and breaks into it twice.

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="phaedo 57a" from="Were you with Socrates yourself" to="from someone else?"}
His question opens the dialogue.
:::

:::passage{ref="phaedo 58d" from="Well, Phaedo, you will have hearers" to="as accurately as you can." lit="hearers who feel as you do"}
He is not listening alone.
:::

## What he does {#moves}

:::moves
1. He asks what Socrates said before his death, and how he died.[^how]
2. He has heard about the trial, and asks why the death came so much later.[^later]
3. He asks which friends were with Socrates, or whether the authorities kept them away.[^friends]
4. When Phaedo tells of the two objections, he breaks in: he sympathizes,[^sympathize] for he too finds himself asking what argument can be believed from now on.[^believe]
5. He must begin over again, he says, and find another argument that the soul does not perish when a man dies.[^another]
6. He asks whether Socrates showed any uneasiness or calmly defended his argument, and whether he defended it successfully.[^calmly]
7. When Simmias and Cebes together accept Socrates’ account of how a philosopher should proceed,[^together] he breaks in again: it seems to him that Socrates made those matters astonishingly clear.[^clear]
:::
