## What’s happening {#happening}

The book said that it is the mind that arranges and causes all things,[^mind] and Socrates was pleased: if so, he thought, the mind arranges each thing as it is best for it to be.[^best] Then, he reasoned, to find the cause of anything a man need examine nothing but what is best.[^examine] He was delighted to think he had found in Anaxagoras a teacher of the cause of things quite to his mind,[^teacher] and he thought Anaxagoras would tell him whether the earth is flat or round,[^earth] and why it is best for the earth to be as it is.[^as] If Anaxagoras made such things clear, Socrates would no longer yearn for any other kind of cause.[^yearn] He seized the books and read them as fast as he could,[^read] but his glorious hope was quickly snatched away,[^hope] for he saw that the man made no use of intelligence.[^intelligence]
