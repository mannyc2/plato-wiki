## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates reminds Simmias that they agreed the soul, if it were a harmony, would follow the elements that compose it and never lead them.[^agreed] Now, he says, they find the soul doing exactly the opposite, leading and opposing the elements it is said to consist of,[^opposite] and speaking to the desires and passions and fears as if it were distinct from them.[^distinct] Homer shows it, he says, when Odysseus smote his breast and chid his heart.[^homer] Asked whether Homer thought of the soul as a harmony led by the body, or as something fitted to lead and rule, far more divine than a harmony,[^divine] Simmias answers the latter.[^latter] So, Socrates concludes, it will never do to say that the soul is a harmony,[^never] or they would agree neither with Homer nor with themselves, and Simmias agrees.[^ourselves] Harmonia, the Theban goddess, has, it seems, been moderately gracious, Socrates says; but how can they find grace in the sight of Cadmus?[^cadmus] Cebes thinks Socrates will find a way, and says his argument against harmony went better than Cebes expected.[^expected]
