## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias laughs, though he does not feel much like laughing just now.[^laugh] The multitude, he says, would say Socrates was quite right about the philosophers,[^multitude] and would add that they know very well the philosophers deserve it;[^deserve] Simmias’ own people at home would agree entirely that philosophers desire death.[^home] Socrates says they would be speaking the truth, except in the matter of knowing very well:[^truth] they do not know in what way real philosophers desire death, nor in what way they deserve it, nor what kind of death it is.[^kind] Paying no further attention to them,[^aside] Socrates asks Simmias whether death is the separation of the soul from the body,[^separation] and being dead the state in which the soul and the body each exist alone by themselves;[^itself] Simmias agrees it is this.[^agree] Asked whether a philosopher would care much about the so-called pleasures of eating and drinking, Simmias says by no means,[^eating] and the same of the pleasures of love.[^love] Socrates goes on to the body’s other cares, such as fine clothes and shoes.[^clothes]
