## What’s happening {#happening}

Since they had their senses from birth, Socrates says, it appears that they must have acquired the knowledge of equality before they were born.[^born] And the argument is no more about the equal than about the beautiful itself, the good itself, the just and the holy,[^beauty] and all the things they stamp as “what it is itself” in their questions and answers.[^seal] If they had not forgotten that knowledge, Socrates says, they would always be born knowing these things and know them throughout life,[^always] for forgetting is the loss of knowledge.[^loss] But if they lost it at birth and later recovered it through the senses, he asks, would not what is called learning be recovering knowledge that is their own, rightly called recollection?[^own] So, Socrates says, one of two things is true: either they are all born knowing these things and know them all their lives, or those who are said to learn merely remember.[^two] He asks Simmias which he chooses.[^choose]
