## What’s happening {#happening}

Phaedo answers that friends were there, a good many,[^many] and he is glad to tell it all, since it is always his greatest pleasure to be reminded of Socrates.[^pleasure] His own feelings that day were strange.[^strange] He was not filled with pity, as he might naturally have been at a friend’s death,[^pity] because Socrates seemed to him happy in his bearing and his words,[^happy] so fearlessly and nobly was he meeting death;[^nobly] Phaedo thought Socrates was not going to the dead without the protection of the gods.[^gods] Nor did Phaedo feel the pleasure he usually took in their talk of philosophy,[^custom] but an unaccustomed mixture of pleasure and pain together.[^mixture] They were all sometimes laughing and sometimes weeping,[^laughing] Apollodorus most of all.[^apollodorus] Phaedo names the Athenians who were there,[^athenians] adding that Plato, he thinks, was ill;[^plato] from abroad came Simmias of Thebes and Cebes, among others.[^foreign] Then Phaedo begins to tell Echecrates everything from the beginning.[^beginning]
