## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias is greater than Socrates not by being Simmias, Socrates says, but by the greatness he happens to have,[^happens] and smaller than Phaedo because Phaedo has greatness relative to Simmias’ smallness.[^phaedo] Socrates laughs that he seems to be speaking like a legal document, but says it really is so.[^legal] Not only will greatness itself never be great and small at once, he says, but the greatness in us will never admit the small;[^admit] when its opposite, smallness, advances, it either withdraws or has already ceased to exist.[^withdraw] So Socrates has received smallness and is still the same small person,[^small] but the greatness in him has not become small.[^great] No other opposite, he says, while still what it was, will ever become or be its own opposite.[^opposite] Cebes finds this quite evident.[^evident] Then someone present, whom Phaedo does not clearly remember,[^someone] asks whether this is not the exact opposite of what they agreed earlier, that opposites are always generated from their opposites.[^earlier] Socrates tells the speaker he has spoken up like a man, but has not observed the difference.[^man] Then, Socrates says, they meant that concrete things are generated from their opposites;[^concrete] now they mean that the opposite itself can never become its own opposite, either in us or in the world.[^itself]
