## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias and Cebes both agree that these are the points,[^points] and say they reject only some of the earlier arguments.[^some] Of the argument that learning is recollection, Cebes says he still believes it more firmly than any other,[^cebes] and Simmias says he feels just as Cebes does.[^simmias] Socrates tells Simmias, calling him his Theban friend, that he must think differently if he persists in holding that a harmony is a compound and that the soul is a harmony.[^theban] Socrates makes the case, and Simmias agrees.

:::moves
1. Simmias will not accept his own statement that a composite harmony existed before the things it had to be composed of.[^before]
2. Yet that is just what he says when he holds that the soul exists before it enters a human body, and that it is composed of things that do not yet exist.[^exists]
3. In a lyre, the lyre, strings and sounds come into being untuned, and the harmony is the last of all to be composed and the first to perish.[^last]
4. So the two theories are not in harmony, and Simmias must choose between them.[^choose]
:::

Simmias chooses recollection. The harmony theory came to him without demonstration and merely seemed probable,[^probable] and arguments that rest on mere probability, he says, are deceptive.[^deceptive] The theory of recollection, by contrast, he says, rests on a hypothesis worth accepting:[^sound] they said that the soul existed before it entered the body, just as the essence that bears the name “what it is” exists.[^essence]
