## What’s happening {#happening}

The soul that has done wrong, Socrates says, wanders alone in utter bewilderment until necessity carries it to its fitting habitation,[^wanders] while the soul that lived in purity and righteousness finds gods for companions and guides.[^gods] The earth itself, he adds, is neither in size nor in other ways what those who discourse about it suppose, as he believes on someone’s authority.[^authority] Simmias has heard a good deal about the earth, but not what Socrates believes, and would like to hear it.[^hear] Socrates thinks telling it would not need the art of Glaucus,[^tell] but proving it true would be too hard even for that art,[^glaucus] and even if he had the skill, he thinks his life would end before such a discussion could be finished.[^life] Still, nothing prevents his telling what he believes the form of the earth to be.[^form] First, he is convinced that if the earth is round and in the middle of the heavens, it needs no air or any other such force to keep it from falling,[^equipoise] but its own equipoise and the homogeneous nature of the heavens on all sides hold it in place;[^homogeneous] and Simmias says rightly so.[^rightly]
