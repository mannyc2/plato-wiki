## What’s happening {#happening}

Phaedo describes a long silence after Socrates finished: Socrates himself seemed absorbed in what had been said, as were most of them.[^silence] Simmias and Cebes talked a little with each other, and Socrates, seeing them, said the argument was still open to doubt and attack for anyone who would discuss it thoroughly,[^attack] and that if they were in any difficulty, they should not hesitate to speak.[^hesitate] Simmias admits that each has been urging the other to ask, but they were reluctant to trouble Socrates in his present misfortune.[^misfortune] Socrates laughs gently:[^laughed] he would have hard work persuading other people that he does not regard his situation as a misfortune, when he cannot even persuade them.[^persuade] Swans, he says, sing most and best when they feel they are to die, in joy that they are going to the god whose servants they are,[^swans] though men, out of their own fear of death, say the swans sing for sorrow.[^sorrow] He counts himself a fellow-servant of the swans, with a gift of prophecy no worse than theirs,[^fellow] and says he leaves life with as little sorrow as they;[^leave] so they may ask whatever they like, so long as the eleven of the Athenians permit.[^eleven]
