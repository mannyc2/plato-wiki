## What’s happening {#happening}

Echecrates asks Phaedo whether he was with Socrates himself on the day Socrates drank the poison,[^there] and Phaedo says he was there himself.[^himself] Echecrates wants to hear what Socrates said before his death and how he died,[^said] since these days none of the Phliasians go to Athens,[^phliasians] and no stranger has come from there who could tell them anything beyond the bare fact that he drank poison and died.[^definite] They had heard about the trial, Echecrates says, and wondered why Socrates was put to death so long after it.[^later] Phaedo says it was a matter of chance:[^chance] the stern of the ship the Athenians send to Delos was crowned the day before the trial.[^crowned] This is the ship, Phaedo explains, in which, as the Athenians say, Theseus once went to Crete with the fourteen youths and maidens,[^theseus] and the Athenians vowed to Apollo, as the story goes, to send a mission to Delos every year if they were saved.[^vow] Their law, he says, requires the city to be pure once the mission begins, so that no one may be publicly executed until the ship has gone to Delos and back;[^pure] the mission begins when the priest of Apollo crowns the stern,[^priest] and that is why Socrates spent a long time in prison between his trial and his death.[^long] Echecrates asks what was said and done at the end, and which of his friends were with him.[^friends]
