## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates draws out what follows, and Simmias agrees at each step.

:::moves
1. Whenever the sight of one thing brings another to mind, whether like it or unlike, that must be recollection.[^recollection]
2. Equal pieces of wood are not equal in the way equality itself is; Simmias says they fall very far short of it.[^short]
3. Whoever sees that a thing aims to be like another but falls short must already know the thing it falls short of.[^previous]
4. So they must have known equality before the time they first saw equal things and thought them falling short of it.[^before]
5. It is through the senses that they come to notice that the things they perceive strive after equality and fall short of it.[^senses]
6. So, if they were to compare those things with equality, they must have gained knowledge of it before they began to see or hear or use the other senses.[^began]
:::

Then Socrates asks whether they did not see and hear and have the other senses as soon as they were born.[^born]

[^recollection]: phaedo 74d "brings you a perception of another, whether they be like or unlike, that must necessarily be recollection"
[^short]: phaedo 74d "They fall very far short of it"
[^previous]: phaedo 74e "must of necessity have previous knowledge of the thing which he says the other resembles but falls short of"
[^before]: phaedo 74e-75a "we must have had knowledge of equality before the time when we first saw equal things"
[^senses]: phaedo 75a-b "it is through the senses that we must learn that all sensible objects strive after absolute equality and fall short of it"
[^began]: phaedo 75b "before we began to see or hear or use the other senses we must somewhere have gained a knowledge of abstract or absolute equality"
[^born]: phaedo 75b "And we saw and heard and had the other senses as soon as we were born?"
