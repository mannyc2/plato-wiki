## What’s happening {#happening}

So such a soul, Socrates says, has no part in communion with the divine and pure.[^communion] This, he tells Cebes, and not the reason the world gives, is why true lovers of knowledge are temperate and brave.[^brave] A philosopher’s soul would not think it right for philosophy to set it free and then to give itself back into bondage to pleasure and pain, like Penelope unweaving the web she wove.[^penelope] It believes it must gain peace from these feelings and follow reason, beholding what is true and divine and not a matter of opinion, and nourished by it;[^food] and it believes it must live so while life lasts, and at death pass on to what is akin to it, free from human ills.[^akin] A soul nurtured in this way, Socrates tells Simmias and Cebes, is not likely to fear that it will be torn apart as it leaves the body and blown away by the winds.[^winds]
