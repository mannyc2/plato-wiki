## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates carries the argument on from what they agreed, and Simmias grants each step.

:::moves
1. One soul is no more or less a soul than another,[^equal] and so, on the harmony view, no more or less a harmony.[^harmony]
2. What is no more or less harmonized has an equal amount of harmony.[^amount]
3. So, if wickedness is discord and virtue harmony, no soul can have more wickedness or virtue than another.[^virtue]
4. Strictly, if the soul is a harmony, no soul will have any wickedness at all.[^none]
5. Then, if all souls are by nature equally souls, all souls of all living creatures will be equally good.[^good]
:::

Asked whether that is true, and whether the reasoning would have come to this end if the soul were a harmony, Simmias answers not in the least.[^least] Socrates then asks whether any part of a man is ruler except the soul, especially a wise one;[^ruler] Simmias thinks not.[^not] And Socrates points to the soul opposing the body, drawing it away from drinking when it is hot and thirsty.[^thirsty]

[^harmony]: phaedo 93d "no less and to no less extent, a harmony than another"
[^amount]: phaedo 93d "An equal amount."
[^virtue]: phaedo 93e "one soul can have no greater amount of wickedness or virtue than another, if wickedness is discord and virtue harmony"
[^none]: phaedo 94a "no soul will have any wickedness at all, if the soul is a harmony"
[^good]: phaedo 94a "if all souls are by nature equally souls, all souls of all living creatures will be equally good"
[^least]: phaedo 94b "Not in the least, he replied"
[^ruler]: phaedo 94b "do you think any is ruler except the soul, especially if it be a wise one"
[^not]: phaedo 94b "No, I do not."
[^thirsty]: phaedo 94b "when the body is hot and thirsty, does not the soul oppose it and draw it away from drinking"
