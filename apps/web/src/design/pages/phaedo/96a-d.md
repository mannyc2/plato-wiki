## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates offers Cebes his own experience, in case anything in it helps with his difficulty,[^experience] and Cebes wishes to hear it.[^hear] When he was young, Socrates says, he was tremendously eager for the kind of wisdom called investigation of nature,[^nature] thinking it a glorious thing to know why each thing comes into being, why it perishes and why it exists.[^glorious] He unsettled himself with questions such as whether it is blood, or air, or fire by which we think.[^questions] In the end he made up his mind that he was by nature totally unfitted for this kind of investigation.[^unfitted] It blinded him so completely that he forgot what he had believed he knew, even about the cause of a man’s growth,[^blinded] which he had thought was plain: from the food he eats, flesh is added to his flesh and bones to his bones.[^growth] Cebes finds that old view reasonable.[^reasonable] Socrates had also been sure that a tall man standing by a short one was taller by a head.[^head]
