## What’s happening {#happening}

The processes between sleeping and waking, Socrates says, are falling asleep and waking up,[^asleep] and he asks Cebes to answer in the same way about life and death.[^tell] Cebes grants that from the living comes the dead,[^dead] and that from the dead he can say only one thing: the living.[^living] Then, Socrates says, our souls exist in the other world, and Cebes answers that so it seems.[^world] Of the two processes between life and death, dying is plain to be seen;[^plain] shall they deny the opposite process, Socrates asks, and make nature one-sided?[^onesided] Cebes says they must grant a process opposite to dying,[^must] and names it: coming to life again.[^again] So, Socrates concludes, the living are generated from the dead just as much as the dead from the living,[^generated] and this seems to him a sufficient proof that the souls of the dead exist somewhere, whence they come back to life.[^proof] Cebes thinks this follows necessarily from what they admitted before,[^necessarily] and Socrates offers another way to show they were right to admit it.[^another]
