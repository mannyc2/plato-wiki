## What’s happening {#happening}

Cebes adds a further argument: if it is true, as Socrates is fond of saying, that our learning is nothing else than recollection,[^recollection] then we must have learned at some previous time what we now remember.[^previous] That would be impossible, Cebes says, if our soul did not exist somewhere before being born in this human form,[^before] so by this argument too the soul appears to be immortal.[^immortal] When Simmias asks to be reminded of the proofs,[^remind] Cebes gives one briefly: people who are questioned well answer correctly of themselves,[^questioned] which they could not do without some knowledge and right reason within them,[^knowledge] as is shown most clearly with mathematical diagrams.[^diagrams] Socrates offers Simmias another way of looking at it,[^way] and Simmias, who says he already begins to recollect from what Cebes said, would still like to hear it.[^begin] Simmias agrees that whoever is to remember anything must have known it at some previous time.[^known] And when a man perceives one thing and also has in mind another, known by a different knowledge, are they not right, Socrates asks, to say that he recollects it?[^recollects] A lover who sees his beloved’s lyre receives in his mind an image of the boy it belongs to,[^lyre] and that, Socrates says, is recollection, just as seeing Simmias often brings Cebes to mind.[^cebes]
