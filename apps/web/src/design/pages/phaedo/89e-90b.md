## What’s happening {#happening}

When this has happened many times, Socrates tells Phaedo, such a man ends by hating everybody and thinking there is nothing sound in anyone at all.[^hating] That is disgraceful, he says, since plainly the man has been dealing with people without any knowledge of human nature.[^nature] With that knowledge he would have thought that the good and the bad are both very few, and those in between very many.[^few] It is as with size, Socrates says: a very large or very small man or dog is rare, and so are the very quick or slow, ugly or beautiful, black or white; the extremes are few and those in between many.[^extremes] Even in a competition in rascality, he says, those who excelled would be very few, and Phaedo thinks it very likely.[^rascality] But arguments are not like men in that respect, Socrates says; he was only following Phaedo’s lead.[^lead] The likeness lies elsewhere: a man without proper knowledge of arguments trusts one as true and later thinks it false, whether it is or not, and this happens again and again.[^again]
