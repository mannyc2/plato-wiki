## What’s happening {#happening}

Such a soul, Socrates says, flits about the monuments and tombs, as the saying goes, where shadowy shapes of souls have been seen,[^tombs] souls not set free in purity, which keep something of the visible.[^visible] Cebes finds this likely,[^likely] and Socrates says it is likely too that these are the souls not of the good but of the base,[^base] wandering until their desire for the bodily imprisons them in a body again.[^again] They are likely to be imprisoned, he says, in natures that match what they practised in life: those given to gluttony, violence and drunkenness pass into asses and beasts of that sort,[^asses] and those who chose injustice, tyranny and robbery into wolves, hawks and kites.[^wolves] Happiest among these, Socrates says, are those who practised the social and civil virtues called moderation and justice from habit, without philosophy or reason;[^habit] they are likely to pass into some social and gentle kind, such as bees, wasps or ants, or into the human race again.[^bees] But no one who has not been a philosopher and does not depart wholly pure, he says, may enter the communion of the gods.[^gods]
