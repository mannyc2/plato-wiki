## What’s happening {#happening}

When soul and body are together, Socrates says, nature directs the body to serve and be ruled and the soul to rule and be master.[^rule] Cebes agrees that the divine is by nature fitted to rule and lead and the mortal to obey and serve,[^fitted] and so that the soul is like the divine and the body like the mortal.[^divine] Socrates draws it together: the soul is most like the divine, immortal, intellectual, uniform, indissoluble and ever unchanging,[^soul] and the body most like the human, mortal, multiform, unintellectual, dissoluble and ever changing.[^body] So, he says, it is natural for the body to dissolve quickly, and for the soul to be entirely indissoluble, or nearly so.[^nearly] Even the body, Socrates points out, does not decay at once after death,[^once] and embalmed, as in Egypt, it stays almost entire for an incalculable time.[^egypt] Would the soul, then, which goes to a place like itself, noble, pure and invisible, to the good and wise god,[^place] be scattered and destroyed as soon as it leaves the body, as most men say?[^scattered] It is there, Socrates adds, that his own soul is soon to go, if God will.[^soon]
