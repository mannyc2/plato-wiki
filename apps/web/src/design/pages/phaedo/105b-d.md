## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates asks Cebes not to answer in the words of the question, but to do as he does:[^begin] instead of the safe but stupid answer that heat makes a body hot, Socrates can now give a more refined one, fire.[^refined] What makes a body ill is not illness but fever,[^fever] and what makes a number odd is not oddness but the number one.[^one] Then the argument runs quickly, with Cebes answering.

:::moves
1. What makes a body alive is the soul,[^soul] always.[^always]
2. Whatever the soul takes possession of, it always brings life to.[^life]
3. Life has an opposite: death.[^death]
4. As they agreed before, the soul will never admit the opposite of what it brings with it.[^admit]
5. What does not admit the even they call uneven.[^uneven]
:::

[^begin]: phaedo 105b "do not answer my questions in their own words, but do as I do"
[^refined]: phaedo 105c "that safe but stupid answer and say that it is heat, but I can now give a more refined answer, that it is fire"
[^fever]: phaedo 105c "I shall not say illness, but fever"
[^one]: phaedo 105c "I shall not say oddness, but the number one"
[^soul]: phaedo 105c "What causes the body in which it is to be alive? The soul, he replied."
[^always]: phaedo 105d "Is this always the case? Yes, said he, of course."
[^life]: phaedo 105d "if the soul takes possession of anything it always brings life to it"
[^death]: phaedo 105d "Is there anything that is the opposite of life? Yes, said he. What? Death."
[^admit]: phaedo 105d "the soul, as we have agreed before, will never admit the opposite of that which it brings with it"
[^uneven]: phaedo 105d "what do we now call that which does not admit the idea of the even? Uneven, said he."
