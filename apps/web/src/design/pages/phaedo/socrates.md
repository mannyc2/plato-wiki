# Socrates

On his last day, Socrates’ friends find him just released from his fetters,[^fetters] and he spends the day arguing that the soul is immortal and imperishable.[^immortal] Phaedo, who was there, says he met death fearlessly and nobly.[^nobly]

:::passage{ref="phaedo 60b" from="But Socrates sat up" to="joined together in one head." lit="What a strange thing"}

## How he argues {#moves}

:::moves
1. Death, he and Simmias agree, is the separation of soul from body,[^separation] and true philosophers practise dying.[^practise]
2. Everything with an opposite comes from its opposite,[^opposites] so the living come from the dead; this seems to him sufficient proof that the souls of the dead exist somewhere.[^proof]
3. All the equal things we sense fall short of equality itself,[^short] so we must have known equality before we were born,[^born] and our souls existed before they were in human form.[^before]
4. The soul is most like the divine, immortal and unchanging, and the body most like the mortal.[^divine]
5. To Simmias: a harmony is the last thing to be composed and the first to perish,[^last] so the theory cannot square with a soul that existed before the body;[^square] and the soul, unlike a harmony, leads the body and opposes it.[^leads]
6. To Cebes: what brings an opposite never admits the opposite of what it brings,[^brings] and the soul always brings life,[^life] so it does not admit death and is immortal.[^deathless]
7. If the soul is immortal, it must be cared for not only in this life but for all time.[^care] Of his story of where souls go, he says no man of sense would insist it is just as he has told it,[^story] but the venture of believing something like it is well worth while.[^venture]
:::

## Also in the Phaedo {#cast}

:::people
- phaedo: Sits at his right hand on a low stool;[^stool] if their argument dies, Socrates tells him, they will both cut their hair today.[^hair]
- simmias: Has doubts and fears, Socrates thinks, that the soul may perish first, being of the nature of a harmony.[^harmony]
- cebes: Granted, as Socrates recalls it, that the soul outlasts the body, but said no one could know it does not perish at last after wearing out many bodies.[^bodies]
- crito: Asks how they shall bury him,[^bury] and at the end closes his mouth and eyes.[^closed]
:::
