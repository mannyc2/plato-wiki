## What’s happening {#happening}

Asked whether a philosopher would care about such adornments or despise them, except so far as it is necessary to have them,[^necessary] Simmias thinks the true philosopher would despise them.[^despise] From there Socrates leads him step by step, and Simmias agrees at each.

:::moves
1. Such a man would, so far as he was able, turn away from the body and concern himself with the soul,[^soul] and in such matters, more than other men, he separates the soul from communion with the body.[^separates]
2. Most people, Socrates notes, think a man who cares nothing for the pleasures of the body is about as good as dead.[^dead]
3. Asking whether the body is a hindrance in the search for wisdom,[^hindrance] Socrates notes that if sight and hearing are not accurate, as the poets are always telling us,[^poets] the other senses are not likely to be, since they are inferior to these.[^inferior]
4. So when the soul tries to consider anything in company with the body, it is evidently deceived by it.[^deceived]
5. In thought, if anywhere, something of the realities becomes clear to the soul,[^thought] and it thinks best when it is, so far as possible, alone by itself and takes leave of the body.[^alone]
6. In this too the philosopher’s soul greatly despises the body and strives to be alone by itself.[^despises]
7. Simmias holds that there is such a thing as absolute justice, and absolute beauty and goodness,[^justice] yet he has never seen anything of that kind with his eyes.[^eyes]
:::
