## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias asks Socrates not to keep his opinion to himself: it is a good that belongs to them in common,[^common] and if it convinces them, it will serve as his defence.[^serve] First, Socrates says, they should ask Crito what he wants.[^crito] Crito reports that the man who is to give the poison has been telling him to warn Socrates to talk as little as possible,[^talk] since, the man says, heat has a bad effect on the action of the poison;[^heat] otherwise he sometimes has to make those who talk too much drink twice or even three times.[^thrice] Socrates tells Crito to let the man do his part and be ready to give it twice, or three times if necessary.[^twice] Then Socrates turns to his friends as his judges,[^judges] to explain why he thinks a man who has really spent his life in philosophy is naturally of good courage when he is to die,[^courage] and has strong hopes of attaining the greatest blessings in that other land.[^blessings] Other people, he says, are likely not to be aware that those who pursue philosophy aright study nothing but dying and being dead.[^dying] If that is true, it would be absurd for such men to be troubled when what they had been practicing for all along at last arrived.[^absurd]
