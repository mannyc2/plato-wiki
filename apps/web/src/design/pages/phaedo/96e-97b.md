## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates had thought, too, that ten were more than eight because two had been added to the eight.[^ten] When Cebes asks what he thinks now,[^now] Socrates says he is far from thinking he knows the cause of any of these things.[^far] He does not even dare say, when one is added to one, which of them has become two.[^two] He marvels that two ones, each one while apart, should become two because they are brought near each other,[^near] and he cannot yet believe that dividing one is what makes it two,[^divide] since that is the opposite of the cause in the first case.[^opposite] So he no longer believes he knows by this method how anything is generated or destroyed or exists,[^method] and has instead another confused way of his own.[^confused] Then one day he heard a man reading from a book, as the man said, by Anaxagoras.[^anaxagoras]
