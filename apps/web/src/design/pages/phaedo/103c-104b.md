## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates asks Cebes whether the objection just raised troubles him too; not this time, Cebes says, though he confesses objections often do.[^troubled] They agree that an opposite can never be its own opposite.[^agreed] Socrates then separates heat from fire and cold from snow.[^snow] Snow, if heat approaches, will never be snow and also warm, but will either withdraw or cease to exist,[^warm] and fire, when cold approaches, will either withdraw or perish.[^fire] In some such cases, Socrates says, something that is not the idea itself always has the idea’s form whenever it exists.[^form] The number three is one: it can always be called by its own name and also be called odd, though odd is not the same as three,[^three] and two and four are each even, though not identical with evenness.[^even] Socrates’ point is that not only do opposites exclude each other, but things that are not opposites of one another but always contain opposites also exclude the idea opposed to the one they contain.[^exclude]
