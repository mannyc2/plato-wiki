## What’s happening {#happening}

Instead of real causes, Socrates says, Anaxagoras named air and ether and water and many other absurdities.[^air] To Socrates it seemed as if someone said that Socrates does with intelligence whatever he does,[^sinews] and then explained that he is sitting here because his body is made of bones and sinews.[^bones] The real causes, Socrates says, are that the Athenians decided it was best to condemn him, and therefore he has decided it is best to sit here and right to stay and undergo whatever penalty they order.[^real] His bones and sinews, he says, would long ago have been in Megara or Boeotia, carried there by an opinion of what was best,[^megara] if he did not think it better and nobler to endure the city’s penalty than to escape and run away.[^nobler] Someone who said he could not do what he thought proper without bones and sinews would be right;[^right] but a cause is one thing, Socrates says, and the thing without which the cause could never be a cause is quite another.[^distinction] Most people, he thinks, who give the name of cause to the second are groping in the dark.[^dark] So, he says, one man makes the earth stay below the heavens with a vortex,[^vortex] and another props it on a foundation of air.[^trough]
