## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates must strive too, he says, because of his impending death,[^death] for he fears that on this question he is not in a philosophical frame of mind but contentious.[^contentious] Uncultured people who argue do not care what the truth is, only that their own views seem true to their hearers;[^hearers] he differs from them, he says, only in being eager above all to make himself believe what he says.[^myself] See how selfish that is, he says: if what he says is true, he gains by believing it,[^selfish] and if there is nothing for him after death, at least he will not burden his friends with lamentations in his last moments.[^burden] So he asks Simmias and Cebes to give little thought to Socrates and much more to the truth,[^truth] and to oppose him with every argument they can muster if he seems wrong,[^oppose] so that he does not deceive himself and them alike and go away like a bee, leaving his sting in them.[^bee] Then he restates the objections: Simmias, he thinks, fears that the soul, though more divine and excellent than the body, may perish first, being of the nature of a harmony;[^first] Cebes granted that the soul lasts longer than the body, but said no one could know that after wearing out many bodies it does not at last perish itself.[^wearing]
