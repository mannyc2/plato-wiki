## What’s happening {#happening}

These mystics, Socrates believes, are those who have been true philosophers,[^mystics] and all his life he has striven in every way to make himself one of them.[^striven] Whether he has striven aright and succeeded, he believes he will know clearly when he arrives there, very soon, if it is God’s will.[^know] This, he tells Simmias and Cebes, is his defence: it is reasonable for him not to grieve at leaving them and the rulers he has here,[^defence] because he believes that there, no less than here, he will find good rulers and friends.[^there] Cebes agrees with the rest,[^agree] but says that about the soul men are very prone to disbelief:[^disbelief] they fear that when it leaves the body it no longer exists anywhere, scattering like a breath or smoke.[^smoke] If it exists somewhere by itself, Cebes says, there would be good reason for the blessed hope,[^hope] but perhaps no little argument and proof is needed to show that after a man is dead his soul still exists and has any power and intelligence.[^proof] Socrates grants that this is true,[^true] and Cebes wants to hear what Socrates thinks about it.[^hear]
