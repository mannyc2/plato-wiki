## What’s happening {#happening}

Such men, Socrates says, conquer pleasures because they are conquered by other pleasures.[^conquer] He suspects that this is not the right way to purchase virtue,[^suspect] by exchanging pleasures for pleasures, pains for pains and fear for fear, as if they were coins,[^coins] and that the only right coinage, for which all those things must be exchanged,[^coinage] is wisdom.[^wisdom] He suspects too that courage, self-restraint, justice and, in short, true virtue exist only with wisdom,[^virtue] whether pleasures and fears are added or taken away;[^added] that virtue made of such exchanges without wisdom is but a painted imitation of virtue, really slavish, with nothing healthy or true in it;[^painted] and that self-restraint, justice, courage and wisdom itself are a kind of purification.[^purification] He fancies that those who established the mysteries had a hidden meaning[^hidden] when they said that whoever goes uninitiated and unsanctified to the other world will lie in the mire, but whoever arrives there initiated and purified will dwell with the gods.[^mire] For, as Socrates quotes the mysteries, the thyrsus-bearers are many, but the mystics few.[^few]
