## What’s happening {#happening}

Simmias says he will tell his difficulty, and then Cebes will say why he does not agree.[^turn] Clear knowledge about these matters, Simmias thinks, as perhaps Socrates does too, is either impossible or very difficult in this life,[^clear] yet a man is a weakling if he does not test what is said about them in every way.[^weakling] He must either learn or discover the truth,[^learn] or, if that is impossible, take the best human doctrine, the hardest to disprove, and sail through life on it as on a raft,[^raft] unless he can make the voyage more safely on some stronger vessel, some divine account.[^vessel] Examined alone or with Cebes, Simmias says, what has been said does not seem quite satisfactory.[^satisfactory] Perhaps he is right, Socrates answers, and asks in what respect.[^respect] Simmias answers that one could use the same argument about a lyre: its harmony is invisible, incorporeal and very beautiful.[^harmony]
