## What’s happening {#happening}

In Phaedo’s report, Socrates asks Simmias whether being reminded is a kind of recollection, especially of things already forgotten through time and inattention, and Simmias agrees.[^forgotten] Socrates’ examples are pictures: a picture of Simmias can remind someone of Cebes,[^cebes] and also of Simmias himself.[^himself] So, Socrates says, recollection is caused both by like things and by unlike things,[^like] and when it is caused by like things, a person inevitably considers whether the likeness is perfect or not.[^perfect] Then Socrates turns to equality: not one piece of wood equal to another, but something beyond all such things.[^beyond] Simmias says most decidedly that there is such a thing,[^is] and, asked whether they know what it is, says certainly.[^know] Equal stones and pieces of wood, though they stay the same, sometimes appear equal to one and not to another, Socrates says;[^appear] but when he asks whether the equals themselves ever appeared unequal, Simmias says never.[^never] So, Socrates concludes, those equal things are not the same as equality itself,[^same] and yet it was from them that Simmias came to know it.[^from]
