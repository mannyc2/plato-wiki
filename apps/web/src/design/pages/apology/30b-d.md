## What’s happening {#happening}

What he urges on everyone, Socrates tells the jury, is to care less for their bodies and property than for the perfection of their souls,[^souls] and he tells them that virtue does not come from money.[^money] If saying these things corrupts the young, he says, then these things must be harmful;[^harmful] and whether the jury acquits him or not, he will not change his conduct, even if he must die many times over.[^many] He asks them not to cry out at what comes next:[^cry] if they kill him, being the man he says he is, they will harm themselves more than him,[^yourselves] for he believes it is not God’s will that a better man be harmed by a worse.[^worse] Neither Meletus nor Anytus could harm him, he says.[^neither] His accuser might perhaps kill him, banish him or disfranchise him, but Socrates thinks the man does himself a far greater injury by what he is doing now, killing a man unjustly.[^unjustly] So he is making his defence, he says, far more for the jurors’ sake than his own, so that they do not, by condemning him, go wrong in their treatment of the god’s gift to them.[^gift]
