## What’s happening {#happening}

Still questioning Meletus in his defence, Socrates now takes up the charge about the gods. Does he corrupt the young, he asks Meletus, by teaching them not to believe in the gods the state believes in, but in other new spiritual beings?[^teaching] Very decidedly, Meletus answers.[^decidedly] Socrates asks him to be clearer: does Meletus say Socrates believes in some gods, only not the state’s, or that he does not believe in gods at all?[^clearer] Meletus says that Socrates does not believe in gods at all.[^atall] Not even that the sun and moon are gods? No, Meletus tells the judges, since Socrates says the sun is a stone and the moon earth.[^stone] Socrates asks whether Meletus thinks he is accusing Anaxagoras, whose books are full of such things,[^anaxagoras] and whose doctrines, Socrates says, the young can buy for a drachma, if the price is high.[^drachma] When Meletus swears again that Socrates believes in no god at all,[^least] Socrates tells him he cannot be believed, not even, it seems, by himself,[^himself] and says Meletus seems to him to have brought the indictment in violence and rashness.[^rashness]
