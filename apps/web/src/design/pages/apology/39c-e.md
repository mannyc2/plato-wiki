## What’s happening {#happening}

To those who condemned him, Socrates now offers a prophecy, for, he says, he is at the time when men most do prophesy, just before death.[^prophesy] Punishment will come on them straight after his death, he says, far more grievous than the death they have given him.[^punishment] They did this, he says, hoping to be freed from giving an account of their lives,[^account] but more people will call them to account, men he has been restraining,[^restrained] and they will be harsher, being younger.[^harsher] Putting men to death, he says, will not stop people from reproaching them; the easiest and most honourable escape is to make themselves as good as possible.[^good] Then he turns to those who voted to acquit him, wanting to talk with them about what has happened,[^acquittal] while the authorities are busy and before he goes to the place where he must die.[^busy]
