## What’s happening {#happening}

Still early in his defence, Socrates explains why he fears the older accusers more than Anytus and the rest.[^fear] They got hold of most of the jurors in childhood,[^childhood] saying, as Socrates reports it, that there is a certain Socrates, a wise man, a ponderer over the things in the air, who makes the weaker argument the stronger.[^charge] Those who hear this, he says, think that men who study such things do not even believe in gods.[^gods] The case, he says, went by default, with nobody to speak for him,[^default] and he cannot even name these accusers, except one who happens to be a writer of comedies.[^comedies] So he must fight with shadows, as he puts it, and cross-question when nobody answers.[^shadows] He will answer these older accusers first, since the jury heard them first.[^first]
