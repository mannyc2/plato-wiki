## What’s happening {#happening}

If they put him to death, Socrates warns in his defence, they will not easily find another like him.[^another] To use what he calls a rather absurd figure, he is like a gadfly on a horse that is large and well bred but sluggish and needs stinging,[^gadfly] and he thinks the god fastened him on the city in some such way, to rouse and reproach each of them all day long.[^fastened] Annoyed like people woken from a nap, they might slap him, as Anytus advises, and easily kill him,[^anytus] and then sleep out the rest of their lives, unless God sent someone else to sting them.[^slumber] That he is the god’s gift, he says, they can see from how he has neglected all his own affairs[^neglect] to come to each of them like a father or an elder brother, urging them to care for virtue.[^virtue] His accusers, he says, have not produced a single witness that he ever asked anyone for pay,[^pay] and he offers his poverty as witness.[^poverty] It may seem strange, he says, that he gives advice in private but does not venture to advise the city in the assembly.[^assembly]
