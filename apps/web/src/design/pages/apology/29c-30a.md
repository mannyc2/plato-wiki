## What’s happening {#happening}

Suppose, Socrates says, the jury were to set aside Anytus, who said Socrates should either never have been tried or, once tried, must certainly be put to death,[^anytus] and let him go on condition that he give up philosophy, on pain of death if caught at it again.[^condition] His answer, as he gives it, would be that he respects and loves them, but will obey the god rather than them,[^obey] and while he lives he will never give up philosophy or stop exhorting them.[^exhort] He would go on asking each person he meets whether he is not ashamed to care for wealth and reputation and honour,[^wealth] while taking no thought for wisdom and truth and the perfection of his soul.[^soul] Anyone who claims to care, Socrates says, he will question and examine, and rebuke if he professes virtue without having it.[^rebuke] He will do this to young and old, citizen and foreigner, but most to the citizens,[^citizens] for the god commands it, he says,[^commands] and he believes no greater good has ever come to the city than his service to the god.[^service]

[^condition]: apology 29c "that you no longer spend your time in this investigation or in philosophy, and if you are caught doing so again you shall die"
[^obey]: apology 29d "I respect and love you, but I shall obey the god rather than you"
[^exhort]: apology 29d "I shall never give up philosophy or stop exhorting you"
[^wealth]: apology 29d-e "not ashamed to care for the acquisition of wealth and for reputation and honor"
[^soul]: apology 29e "when you neither care nor take thought for wisdom and truth and the perfection of your soul?"
[^rebuke]: apology 29e "if I find that he does not possess virtue, but says he does, I shall rebuke him"
[^citizens]: apology 30a "young and old, foreigner and citizen, but most to the citizens"
[^commands]: apology 30a "the god commands me to do this"
[^service]: apology 30a "no greater good ever came to pass in the city than my service to the god"
