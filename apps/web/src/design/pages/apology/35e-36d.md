## What’s happening {#happening}

The jury has voted to condemn him, and Socrates begins his second speech, on the penalty.[^vote] He is not grieved, he says, and the verdict was no surprise;[^surprise] what surprises him is how close the vote was, for if only thirty votes had gone the other way he would have been acquitted.[^thirty] As far as Meletus is concerned, he claims, he has been acquitted even now,[^meletus] for without Anytus and Lycon, Meletus would have been fined a thousand drachmas for not getting a fifth of the votes.[^fifth] His accuser proposes death,[^death] so Socrates must propose an alternative: clearly, he says, what he deserves.[^deserve] He has neglected, he says, what most men care for, money-making and offices,[^neglect] to try to persuade each citizen to care for himself and his goodness and wisdom more than for his belongings.[^care] Such a man, he says, deserves some good thing,[^good] and nothing is so fitting for a poor benefactor who needs leisure to exhort them as meals in the prytaneum,[^prytaneum] more fitting than for an Olympic victor in the horse races, who makes them seem happy, while Socrates makes them happy in reality.[^olympic]
