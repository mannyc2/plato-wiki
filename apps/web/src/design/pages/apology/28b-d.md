## What’s happening {#happening}

Still in his defence, Socrates imagines someone asking whether he is not ashamed of a pursuit that has put him in danger of death.[^ashamed] His reply, as he gives it, is that a man of any worth should weigh only whether what he does is right or wrong, the act of a good man or a bad one, not the risk of life or death.[^weigh] Otherwise, he says, the demigods who died at Troy would be bad, among them the son of Thetis (Achilles), who so despised danger.[^thetis] His mother, a goddess, had told him, Socrates recalls, that if he killed Hector his own death would follow,[^die] and he feared much more to live as a coward and not avenge his friends.[^coward] Wherever a man is stationed, Socrates says, by his own choice or by his commander, there it seems to him that he must stay and run his risks,[^remain] counting nothing, death included, worse than disgrace.[^disgrace]
