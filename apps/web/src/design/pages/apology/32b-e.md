## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates’ first proof from his own life: the only office he ever held was senator,[^senator] and when his tribe presided and the Athenians wished to judge together, not one by one, the ten generals who had failed to gather up the dead after the sea battle,[^generals] something, he says, they all agreed afterwards was illegal,[^illegal] he alone of the presiding prytanes opposed it.[^alone] The orators were ready to impeach and arrest him, and the people shouted them on,[^shouts] but he thought he must run the risk with law and justice on his side.[^risk] The second: under the oligarchy, the Thirty ordered him and four others to bring Leon the Salaminian from Salamis to be put to death;[^leon] the other four went, and Socrates simply went home.[^home] So he showed by action, he says, that he did not care a whit for death but cared with all his might to do nothing unjust or unholy,[^unholy] and perhaps he would have been put to death for it, had that government not soon been put down.[^died] Could he have lived so many years if he had been in public life and acted as a good man should?[^years] Far from it, he tells the jury.[^far]
