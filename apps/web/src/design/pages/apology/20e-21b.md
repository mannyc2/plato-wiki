## What’s happening {#happening}

Explaining in his defence where his reputation comes from, Socrates asks the jury not to interrupt if he seems to boast,[^boast] for, he says, he will offer the god of Delphi as witness to his wisdom, if it is wisdom at all.[^delphi] Socrates tells how his old comrade Chaerephon went to Delphi and asked whether anyone was wiser than Socrates;[^asked] the Pythia, Socrates says, replied that no one was wiser.[^pythia] Since Chaerephon is dead, Socrates says, his brother, present in court, will bear witness to this.[^brother] Socrates says he asked himself what the god could mean, what riddle he was setting,[^riddle] since he was conscious of not being wise, much or little;[^wise] yet, he reasoned, the god cannot be lying, for that is not possible for him.[^lying] For a long time he was at a loss, and then, with great reluctance, he set out to investigate.[^reluctance]
