## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates opens his defence by telling the jury that his accusers spoke so persuasively he almost forgot who he was,[^persuasive] though hardly a word they said is true.[^truth] What amazed him most was their warning that the jury must not be deceived by him, because he was a clever speaker.[^clever] He is not one, he says, unless they call a clever speaker one who speaks the truth.[^speaks] They will hear from him, he says, things said at random, in whatever words occur to him,[^random] and he begs them to bear with him if he defends himself in the words he has always used in the market place,[^market] since this is his first time before a court, at seventy years old.[^seventy] He asks them to attend only to whether what he says is just: that, he says, is the virtue of a judge, as speaking the truth is an orator’s.[^virtue] He will answer the first accusations and the first accusers before the later ones.[^order]

[^truth]: apology 17a "there is hardly a word of truth in what they have said"
[^clever]: apology 17a-b "you must be on your guard not to be deceived by me, because I was a clever speaker"
[^speaks]: apology 17b "unless indeed they call him a clever speaker who speaks the truth"
[^random]: apology 17c "you will hear things said at random with the words that happen to occur to me"
[^market]: apology 17c "making my defence with the same words with which I have been accustomed to speak both in the market place"
[^seventy]: apology 17d "this is the first time I have come before the court, although I am seventy years old"
[^virtue]: apology 18a "that is the virtue of a judge, and an orator’s virtue is to speak the truth"
[^order]: apology 18a "defend myself against the first false accusations brought against me, and the first accusers"
