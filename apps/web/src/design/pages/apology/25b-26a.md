## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates tests Meletus’ answer against horses: only one man or very few, the horse-trainers, can make them better, he says, while most people who handle them do them harm,[^horses] and so it is with all other animals.[^animals] By his answers, Socrates tells Meletus, he shows that he never thought about the young.[^never] Next Socrates asks whether bad people do harm to those near them and good people good; Meletus agrees,[^certainly] and agrees that no one wants to be harmed.[^injured] Does Socrates, then, corrupt the young voluntarily or involuntarily? Voluntarily, says Meletus.[^voluntarily] Socrates replies that he is not so ignorant as not to know that making his companions bad puts him in danger of harm from them.[^danger] So either he does not corrupt them, he says, or he does it involuntarily,[^either] and for involuntary errors the law calls for private instruction and warning, not a court.[^private]
