## What’s happening {#happening}

Still in his defence, Socrates tells the jury that all his life, in private and in public, if he did anything in public,[^public] he has been the same, and has never yielded wrongly to anyone,[^same] including those his slanderers call his pupils.[^pupils] But he was never anyone’s teacher, he says.[^teacher] He talks with rich and poor alike, never for pay, and whoever wishes may answer his questions and hear what he says.[^poor] So, he says, he cannot justly be held responsible for how any of them turns out,[^responsible] and anyone who claims to have learned something from him in private that the others did not hear is lying.[^lying] Why then do some love to spend so much time with him? Because, he says, they enjoy hearing people examined who think they are wise and are not.[^amusing] And he believes he was commanded to do this by the god, through oracles and dreams and in every way a divine power ever commanded anyone.[^commanded]
