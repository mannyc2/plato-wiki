## What’s happening {#happening}

From this investigation, Socrates says, came many harsh enmities,[^enmities] and the name of a wise man, because onlookers think he is wise in whatever he refutes in others.[^name] But it is likely, he says, that the god is really wise and means by his oracle that human wisdom is of little or no value,[^value] using Socrates’ name only as an example: wisest is whoever, like Socrates, recognizes that he is of no account in respect to wisdom.[^example] So, he says, he still goes about investigating at the god’s behest anyone he thinks wise,[^behest] and when the man does not seem wise to him, he comes to the god’s aid and shows that he is not.[^show] This leaves him no leisure, he says, for any of the city’s affairs worth mentioning, or for his own, and he lives in vast poverty.[^poverty] Rich young men with leisure follow him of their own accord, often imitate him, and examine others in turn;[^imitate] and those they examine, Socrates says, are angry with him instead of with themselves.[^angry]
