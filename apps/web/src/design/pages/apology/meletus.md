# Meletus

One of Socrates’ three accusers, with Anytus and Lycon,[^three] and the man who brought the indictment.[^indictment] Socrates calls him up to answer questions before the jury, and argues that Meletus contradicts himself, as if he were to say that Socrates does wrong by not believing in gods, but believing in gods.[^contradict]

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="apology 24b" from="but against Meletus" to="defend myself next." lit="the good and patriotic, as he says"}
Socrates turns to him once the older accusations are answered.
:::

:::passage{ref="apology 24c-d" from="Come here, Meletus" to="I do." lit="Come here, Meletus"}
Called up to answer, he agrees that it matters greatly for the young to be as good as possible.
:::

## How he argues {#moves}

:::moves
1. The laws make the young better, he says;[^laws] asked what man, he names the judges,[^judges] all of them.[^all]
2. He agrees that all the Athenians make the young good except Socrates, who alone corrupts them.[^alone]
3. Socrates corrupts them voluntarily, he says.[^voluntarily]
4. He agrees that Socrates corrupts them by teaching them not to believe in the gods the state believes in, but in other new spiritual beings.[^teaching]
5. He says Socrates does not believe in gods at all,[^atall] since Socrates says the sun is a stone and the moon earth.[^stone]
6. Forced by the jurors, by Socrates’ account, he grants that whoever believes in spiritual things believes in spirits;[^spirits] he also agrees that spirits are gods or children of gods.[^children]
7. He proposes death as the penalty,[^death] as Socrates names him later in the same speech.[^proposes]
:::
