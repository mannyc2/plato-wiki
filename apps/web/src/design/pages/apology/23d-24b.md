## What’s happening {#happening}

Those who are examined, Socrates says, call him a corrupter of the youth,[^corrupting] and when asked by doing or teaching what, they have nothing to say.[^what] So they fall back on what is handy to say against all philosophers: things in the air and beneath the earth, not believing in the gods, making the weaker argument the stronger.[^handy] They have long filled the jury’s ears with slanders, he says.[^slanders] From among them, Socrates says, came Meletus, Anytus and Lycon:[^three] Meletus on the poets’ account, Anytus on the artisans’ and public men’s,[^poets] Lycon on the orators’.[^orators] He knows well enough that speaking so openly makes him hated, which he takes as proof that he speaks the truth.[^proof] That, he says, is defence enough against the first accusers;[^enough] next he turns to Meletus, good and patriotic by his own account,[^patriotic] and to the formal charge, as Socrates states it: that he corrupts the youth and does not believe in the gods the state believes in, but in other new spiritual beings.[^charge]
