## What’s happening {#happening}

When the commanders the jury chose stationed him at Potidaea, Amphipolis and Delium, Socrates says, he stayed and risked death like anyone else.[^stations] It would be terrible, then, if he deserted the station the god gave him, as he believed, to spend his life in philosophy, examining himself and others.[^philosophy] Then one might justly bring him to court for not believing in gods, since he would be disobeying the oracle and fearing death.[^court] To fear death, he says, is nothing but thinking one is wise when one is not:[^wise] no one knows whether death is not even the greatest of all blessings, yet people fear it as if they knew it were the greatest of evils.[^blessings] If he is wiser than others in anything, Socrates says, it is that, not knowing much about the other world, he does not think he knows.[^other] What he does know is that it is evil and disgraceful to do wrong and to disobey one who is better, whether god or man.[^know] So he will never fear or avoid what he does not know to be good or bad rather than what he knows is bad.[^fear]
