## What’s happening {#happening}

Defending himself now against the formal charge, Socrates answers it with a charge of his own: Meletus is the wrongdoer, he says, joking in earnest and dragging people into court,[^joke] pretending to care about things he never cared about at all.[^pretending] Then he calls Meletus up to answer questions before the jury.[^come] Meletus agrees that it matters greatly for the young to be as good as possible,[^good] so Socrates asks who makes them better, and when Meletus is silent,[^silent] calls that proof that he has never cared.[^proof] The laws, Meletus answers;[^laws] and when Socrates asks what man, Meletus says the judges,[^judges] all of them.[^all] The listeners make them better too, Meletus says, and the senators,[^senators] and the members of the assembly.[^assembly] So all the Athenians, Socrates sums up, make the young good except himself, and he alone corrupts them; very decidedly, Meletus answers, that is what he means.[^decidedly]
