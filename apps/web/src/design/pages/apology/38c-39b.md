## What’s happening {#happening}

The jury has sentenced him to death,[^sentenced] and Socrates speaks a last time, first to those who voted for his death.[^those] They gain little time, he says, and for it those who wish to revile the city will blame them for killing Socrates, a wise man;[^wise] the revilers will call him wise, he adds, even though he is not.[^notwise] Had they waited a little, what they wanted would have come of itself, since he is old and near death.[^waited] He was convicted, he says, not for lack of words but for lack of shamelessness,[^lack] since he would not wail and lament as they are used to hearing others do.[^wailing] He does not repent of his defence, and much prefers to die after such a defence than to live after the other kind.[^prefer] It is not hard to escape death, he says; it is much harder to escape wickedness, which runs faster than death.[^faster] Slow and old, he says, he has been caught by the slower runner, and his accusers, clever and quick, by the faster.[^caught] He abides by his penalty, and they by theirs, and perhaps, he says, it had to be so.[^abide]
