## What’s happening {#happening}

Meletus agrees that spirits are gods or children of gods.[^certainly] If spirits are gods, Socrates argues, that is the joke Meletus is making: Socrates does not believe in gods, yet believes in gods, since he believes in spirits.[^joke] If spirits are children of gods by nymphs or others, what man would believe in children of gods but not in gods?[^children] That would be as absurd, he says, as believing in mules but not in horses and asses.[^mules] So, Socrates tells Meletus, he brought this suit either to test them or for want of a true wrong to charge,[^test] and, he says, there is no way to persuade anyone with even a little sense[^sense] that the same man believes in spiritual and divine things but not in spirits or gods or heroes.[^heroes] Against Meletus’ indictment, Socrates says, he needs no long defence;[^enough] what will condemn him, if anything does, is not Meletus or Anytus but the prejudice and dislike of the many,[^many] which has condemned many other good men.[^others]
