## What’s happening {#happening}

Nor is it true, Socrates goes on in his defence, that he teaches people and makes money by it,[^money] though it would seem a fine thing, he says, if one might be able to teach people as Gorgias of Leontini, Prodicus of Ceos and Hippias of Elis are.[^fine] Each of them, he says, can persuade the young to leave their own fellow citizens, associate with them instead, pay them, and be grateful besides.[^pay] He tells how he once asked Callias, a man who has spent more on sophists than all the rest,[^callias] whom he would hire for his two sons: for colts there would be a horse-trainer, but who has knowledge of the excellence of a man and a citizen?[^citizen] Callias named Evenus of Paros, at five minae.[^evenus] Socrates called Evenus blessed, if he really had this art;[^art] he himself does not understand such things.[^understand] Then he imagines someone asking where, in that case, all this prejudice against him came from,[^whence] and he grants that the man is right to ask.[^right] His reputation, he says, comes from a sort of wisdom, perhaps human wisdom.[^human]
