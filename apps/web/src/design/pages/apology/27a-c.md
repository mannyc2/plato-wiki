## What’s happening {#happening}

Meletus, Socrates tells the jury, seems to be testing him with a puzzle,[^puzzle] for he appears to contradict himself, as if he were saying that Socrates does wrong by not believing in gods, but believing in gods.[^contradict] Socrates asks the jurors to examine this with him, and not to make a disturbance at his accustomed way of arguing.[^manner] Does anyone believe in things pertaining to horses but not in horses, or in things pertaining to flute-players but not in flute-players?[^flute] When Meletus will not answer, Socrates answers for him: no one does.[^there] Does anyone believe in spiritual things but not in spirits? This time Meletus answers that no one does,[^spirits] reluctantly, Socrates says, and only when the jurors force him.[^forced] But Meletus swore in his indictment that Socrates believes in spiritual beings, Socrates says,[^swore] and then it is quite inevitable that he believes in spirits too.[^inevitable]

[^there]: apology 27b "There is not, best of men; if you do not wish to answer, I say it to you and these others here."
