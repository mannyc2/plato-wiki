## What’s happening {#happening}

In his last words, Socrates speaks to those who voted to acquit him as friends,[^friends] and calls them judges, which he says is their right name.[^judges] Something wonderful has happened to him, he tells them.[^wonderful] His customary prophetic sign, he says, used to oppose him even in very small matters, when he was about to do something he should not,[^small] yet today it did not oppose him when he left home, when he came to court, or at any point in his speech.[^speech] So, he says, what has happened to him is doubtless a good thing,[^good] and those who think death an evil must be mistaken;[^mistaken] the sign would surely have opposed him had he not been going to meet with something good.[^sign] He gives a second reason to hope: death, he says, is one of two things,[^two] either a kind of nothingness, in which the dead have no consciousness of anything,[^nothing] or, as people say, a change and migration of the soul from here to another place.[^migration]
