## What’s happening {#happening}

Continuing his defence, Socrates tells how he went first to one of the public men with a reputation for wisdom,[^public] thinking that there, if anywhere, he would prove the oracle wrong.[^wrong] The man, Socrates says, seemed to him to seem wise to many people and above all to himself, but not to be so;[^seem] and when Socrates tried to show him this, the man and many of those present came to hate Socrates.[^hateful] Going away, Socrates reasoned that neither of them really knows anything fine and good,[^neither] but that he seemed to be wiser in just this little thing: what he does not know, he does not think he knows.[^little] He went to another man reputed wiser still, and there the same things seemed to him true.[^same] So he went from one to another, seeing that he was hated, grieving and afraid,[^grieving] yet thinking he must put the god’s business above everything.[^business]
