## What’s happening {#happening}

In his defence Socrates must try, in a short time, to remove a prejudice the jury has been acquiring for so long,[^short] and he thinks it difficult;[^difficult] still, he says, let it go as pleases God; the law must be obeyed.[^law] He reads out the older accusers’ charge as if they were plaintiffs with a sworn statement:[^sworn] Socrates, it runs, is a criminal and a busybody, investigating the things beneath the earth and in the heavens,[^busybody] making the weaker argument stronger and teaching others the same.[^teaching] The jury saw this for themselves, he says, in Aristophanes’ comedy, where a Socrates is carried about proclaiming that he treads on air.[^comedy] He means no dishonour to such knowledge, if anyone is wise about such matters,[^dishonor] but he has nothing to do with these things.[^nothing] He calls most of the jurors as his witnesses: let them tell one another if anyone ever heard him talk about such matters, much or little.[^witnesses]
