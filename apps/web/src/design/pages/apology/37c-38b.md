## What’s happening {#happening}

Still in his speech on the penalty, Socrates weighs what else he might propose. Why should he live in prison, a slave to whoever holds office?[^prison] A fine, with prison until it is paid, comes to the same thing, since he has no money.[^money] Exile the jury might perhaps accept,[^exile] but if his own fellow citizens could not endure his talk, others will not, and he would wander from city to city, always being driven out.[^wander] Someone might ask whether he could not go away and live quietly, without talking.[^quietly] That, he says, is the hardest thing to make some of them believe: if he says it would be disobedience to the god, they will think he is jesting,[^jest] and if he says that talking every day about virtue is the greatest good for a man, and that the unexamined life is not worth living, they will believe him still less.[^unexamined] He could pay perhaps a mina of silver, and proposes that;[^mina] but Plato, Crito, Critobulus and one other[^friends] urge him to propose thirty minas, and say they will stand surety for it, so he proposes that amount.[^thirty]
