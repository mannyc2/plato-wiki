## What’s happening {#happening}

To examine the leader of the great army against Troy, or Odysseus, or Sisyphus, would be immeasurable happiness, Socrates says,[^happiness] and there, at any rate, they do not kill people for it, being immortal, if what we are told is true.[^immortal] The judges who acquitted him, he says, must also regard death hopefully,[^hope] holding to one truth: no evil can come to a good man, in life or after death, and the gods do not neglect his affairs.[^truth] What has come to him did not come by chance, he says: it was better for him to die now, which is why the sign never stopped him,[^better] and he is not at all angry with those who condemned and accused him,[^angry] though they meant to harm him and deserve blame for that.[^blame] Yet he makes one request of them: when his sons grow up, if the sons seem to care for money or anything else more than for virtue,[^virtue] he asks that they be punished, troubled and rebuked as he has troubled and rebuked his hearers.[^sons] If this is done, he says, he and his sons will have been justly treated.[^just] Now it is time to go, he to die and his hearers to live; which of the two goes to the better lot, he says, is known to none but God.[^god]
