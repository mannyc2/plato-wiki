## What’s happening {#happening}

The reason, Socrates tells the jury, is one they have often heard from him: something divine and spiritual comes to him, the very thing Meletus ridiculed in his indictment.[^divine] He has had it, he says, since childhood:[^childhood] a sort of voice which, when it comes, always holds him back from what he is about to do, and never urges him on.[^voice] It is this that opposes his going into politics,[^politics] and he thinks the opposition a very good thing, for had he gone into politics, he says, he would have been put to death long ago,[^death] and done no good to the jury or to himself.[^good] No man will save his life, he says, who nobly opposes them or any other populace and stops many unjust and illegal things from happening in the city.[^save] A man who really fights for the right, if he is to survive even a little while, must be a private citizen, not a public man.[^private] He offers to prove this not with words, he says, but with actions, which the jury honours more.[^actions]
