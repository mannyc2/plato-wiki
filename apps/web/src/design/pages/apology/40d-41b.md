## What’s happening {#happening}

Still speaking to those who voted to acquit him, Socrates takes the first possibility: if death is like a sleep so deep that the sleeper does not even dream, he says, it would be a wonderful gain.[^gain] If anyone were to compare a night of dreamless sleep with the other days and nights of his life, not only a private person, Socrates believes, but even the great King himself would find few that passed more pleasantly.[^king] Then all time would seem no longer than one night.[^night] But if death is a change of habitation to another place, and what we are told is true, that all the dead are there, what greater blessing could there be?[^blessing] There, he says, one would find the true judges who are said to sit in judgement there, Minos and Rhadamanthus, Aeacus and Triptolemus,[^minos] and could meet Orpheus, Musaeus, Hesiod and Homer;[^homer] he is willing to die many times over, if these things are true.[^die] He would compare his lot with that of Palamedes, Ajax son of Telamon, and others who died through an unjust judgement,[^palamedes] and his greatest pleasure would be to go on examining people there as here, to find out who is wise and who only thinks he is.[^examine]
