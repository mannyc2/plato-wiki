## What’s happening {#happening}

Continuing his defence, Socrates swears by the Dog that those with the most reputation seemed to him almost the most deficient, and men of less repute better at being sensible.[^dog] He recounts his wandering, he says, so that the oracle might be proved irrefutable.[^irrefutable] After the public men he went to the poets, and hardly anyone present, one might say, would not have spoken better than they about their own poems.[^poems] What they compose, he concluded, they compose not by wisdom but by nature and inspiration,[^inspired] and because of their poetry they thought themselves the wisest of men in other things too.[^other] Last he went to the hand-workers, and found that they did know what he did not, and in that were wiser than he;[^artisans] but because each practised his art well, each thought himself very wise in the other most important matters.[^failing] Asking himself, on the oracle’s behalf, whether he would rather be as he is or as they are, he answered that it was better to be as he is.[^better]
