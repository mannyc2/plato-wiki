## What’s happening {#happening}

If he had been corrupting the young, Socrates argues, some of them, now older, would have come forward to accuse him,[^older] or their fathers, brothers or other kinsfolk would speak now.[^kin] Many of these are in court, and he names several: Crito, of his own age and deme, father of Critobulus;[^crito] Lysanias the Sphettian, father of Aeschines;[^lysanias] and brothers of young men who joined his conversations, among them Adimantus, whose brother is Plato.[^plato] Meletus ought to have produced one of them as a witness, Socrates says, and he yields the floor if Meletus has any such testimony now.[^floor] Instead, he says, they are all ready to help him, the man who by Meletus’ and Anytus’ account corrupts their relatives.[^aid] The older relatives, not corrupted themselves, can have only one reason, he says: they know Meletus is lying and Socrates is telling the truth.[^truth] That, Socrates says, is about all he has to say in his defence.[^defence]
