## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates presses Euthyphro to tell the truth: Euthyphro knows, if anyone does, Socrates says, and like Proteus must be held until he speaks.[^proteus] Had Euthyphro not known clearly what is holy and unholy, Socrates says, he would never have undertaken to prosecute his aged father for murder on a hired man’s account;[^father] he would have feared the gods’ anger in case he was wrong, and been ashamed before men.[^afraid] Socrates is sure that Euthyphro thinks he knows,[^sure] and asks him not to conceal what he thinks.[^conceal] Euthyphro answers: some other time; he is in a hurry, and it is time for him to go.[^hurry] As Euthyphro leaves, Socrates has the last words: he had hoped to be rid of Meletus’ indictment by showing that Euthyphro had made him wise about divine matters,[^meletus] and that he would live a better life from now on.[^better] Instead Euthyphro goes, leaving Socrates cast down from his hope of learning what is holy and what is not, and the dialogue ends with that question unanswered.[^hope]
