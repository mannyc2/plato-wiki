## What’s happening {#happening}

Perhaps, Socrates goes on, Meletus is first clearing away those who, as Meletus says, corrupt the young plants, Socrates among them;[^clearing] after that, when he has turned to the older men, he will, as is natural, bring the city countless most precious blessings.[^blessings] Euthyphro fears the opposite: that in going after Socrates, Meletus is harming the city at its very heart.[^heart] Socrates gives the charge as Meletus puts it: that Socrates makes new gods and does not believe in the old ones.[^charge] Euthyphro says he understands: it is because Socrates says the divine monitor keeps coming to him.[^monitor] People laugh at Euthyphro too, he says, and call him crazy when he speaks in the assembly about divine things and foretells the future,[^laugh] though not one of his predictions, he says, has failed to come true.[^true] Socrates answers that the Athenians, as he supposes, do not much mind a man they think clever, so long as he does not impart his cleverness to others;[^impart] they grow angry when they think he makes others like himself.[^angry] He fears that, because of his love of men, they think he pours himself out to anyone and everyone without payment.[^payment]
