## What’s happening {#happening}

Asked what kind of attention he means, Euthyphro answers: the kind slaves pay their masters.[^servants] Socrates takes this to mean a kind of service to the gods, and Euthyphro agrees.[^exactly] Socrates asks what such service helps produce: the art that serves physicians, health;[^health] the art that serves shipbuilders, a ship;[^ship] the art that serves housebuilders, a house.[^house] Euthyphro, Socrates reminds him, says he knows more than any other man about the gods,[^claim] and Euthyphro holds to it.[^true] Then what, Socrates asks, is the glorious result the gods accomplish by using us as servants?[^glorious] Euthyphro answers that they accomplish many fine results.[^many] So do generals, Socrates replies, yet their chief result is easy to name, victory in war,[^victory] as the chief result of farming is food from the land.[^food] So what is the chief result of the gods’ work?[^chief]
