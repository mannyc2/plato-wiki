## What’s happening {#happening}

Euthyphro gives his reason: knowingly keeping company with a killer brings the same pollution, unless one purifies oneself and him by prosecuting.[^pollution] By his account of what happened, a hired workman of his, working the family’s land on Naxos,[^naxos] got drunk and killed one of their house slaves.[^slave] Euthyphro’s father bound the man hand and foot, threw him into a ditch, and sent to Athens to ask the religious adviser what to do.[^ditch] Meanwhile the father neglected him, and the man died of hunger, cold and his bonds before the messenger came back.[^died] Euthyphro’s father and relatives are angry with him;[^angry] they say the father did not kill the man, and that even if he had, the dead man was a murderer and not worth troubling about;[^kill] and that it is unholy for a son to prosecute his father for murder;[^unholy] that, says Euthyphro, shows how little they know of the divine law about holiness.[^little] Socrates asks whether Euthyphro’s knowledge of these things is so exact that he is not afraid of doing something unholy himself.[^afraid]
