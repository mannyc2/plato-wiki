## What’s happening {#happening}

We have nothing good that the gods do not give, Socrates says;[^give] but what advantage do they get from what they receive from us?[^advantage] Surely Socrates does not suppose, Euthyphro asks, that the gods gain any advantage from it?[^suppose] Asked what our gifts to the gods are, then, Euthyphro answers honour, gifts of honour and, as he said before, gratitude.[^honour] When Socrates asks whether holiness is then gratifying to the gods, but not advantageous or precious to them,[^grateful] Euthyphro thinks it precious above all things.[^precious] Then holiness, Socrates says, is again, it seems, what is precious to the gods,[^again] and Euthyphro agrees.[^certainly] Socrates asks whether Euthyphro will blame him as the Daedalus who makes Euthyphro’s words walk, when Euthyphro himself is much more skilful than Daedalus and makes them go round in a circle.[^circle]

Then Socrates lays out the circle, and Euthyphro grants each step.

:::moves
1. A while ago they found that holiness and what is dear to the gods are not the same, but different.[^before]
2. Now Euthyphro says that what is precious to the gods is holy, and what is precious to the gods is what is dear to them.[^dear]
3. So either the earlier agreement was wrong, or, if it was right, they are wrong now.[^either]
:::

To the last step Euthyphro says, “So it seems.”[^seems] They must begin again at the beginning, Socrates says, and ask what holiness is,[^begin] for he will not willingly give up until he learns.[^giveup]
