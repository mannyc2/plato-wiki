# Socrates

Indicted by Meletus for impiety,[^impiety] Socrates meets Euthyphro at the portico of the king archon[^portico] and asks to become his pupil in what holiness is.[^pupil] By the end, Euthyphro has gone and Socrates has not learned it.[^hope]

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="euthyphro 2a" from="What strange thing" to="king archon sits?" lit="the portico where the king archon sits"}
Far from his usual haunts, he has come to answer an indictment.
:::

:::passage{ref="euthyphro 15e-16a" from="Oh my friend" to="better life henceforth." lit="cast down from the high hope I had"}
Euthyphro has hurried off, and the question is still open.
:::

## How he argues {#moves}

:::moves
1. He asks not for one or two holy acts, but for the one aspect by which all holy acts are holy,[^aspect] to use as a model.[^model]
2. He draws out what an answer implies: if the gods disagree about right and wrong, the same things would be both holy and unholy.[^both]
3. He asks whether the holy is loved by the gods because it is holy, or holy because it is loved.[^question]
4. He separates what holiness is from something that has happened to it, such as being loved by the gods.[^happened]
5. When Euthyphro is stuck, Socrates offers to help,[^aid] and proposes that holiness is a part of the right.[^part]
6. He tests “attention to the gods” against arts such as horsemanship.[^horses]
7. He will not willingly give up until he learns.[^giveup]
:::
