## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates turns to men, and asks whether Euthyphro has ever heard anyone argue that a wrongful killer ought not to pay the penalty.[^heard] Then he asks whether such people admit their wrong and still say they should not pay, and Euthyphro grants that they do not.[^admit] What they dispute, Socrates says, is perhaps who did wrong, what he did, and when,[^when] and Euthyphro agrees.[^true] The same holds for the gods, Socrates says, if they quarrel: no one, god or man, has the face to say that a wrongdoer ought not to pay,[^face] and Euthyphro grants it in the main.[^main] Those who dispute, Socrates thinks, men and gods alike, if the gods do dispute, dispute about each separate act.[^act] So Socrates asks for Euthyphro’s proof that all the gods think the workman died wrongfully,[^proof] and that it is right for a son to prosecute his father on such a man’s account.[^right]

## Two views {#views}

:::views
- euthyphro: People are always arguing these points in the law courts; they will do or say anything to escape the penalty.[^courts]
- socrates: No one, he fancies, dares argue that one who has really done wrong ought not to pay the penalty.[^dare]
:::
