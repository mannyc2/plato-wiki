## What’s happening {#happening}

Euthyphro finds Socrates far from his usual haunts in the Lyceum, at the portico where the king archon sits.[^portico] Euthyphro says he has an action of his own before the king, and supposes Socrates cannot.[^action] Socrates corrects him: the Athenians call his case not an action but an indictment.[^indictment] Socrates hardly knows the man who brought it, who seems young and unknown; his name, Socrates believes, is Meletus.[^meletus] Meletus, Socrates says, claims to know how the young are corrupted and who corrupts them,[^knows] so he must be a wise man.[^wise] Socrates adds that Meletus seems to him the only public man who begins in the right way, caring first for the young,[^young] as a good farmer tends the young plants first.[^plants]
