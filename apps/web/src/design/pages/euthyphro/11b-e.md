## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates says Euthyphro has not yet said what holiness really is,[^yet] and asks him to begin again and say what it is, whether it is loved by the gods or has anything else happen to it, for they will not quarrel about that.[^again] Euthyphro says he does not know how to say what he means: whatever they put forward moves about and will not stay where they put it.[^stay] Socrates likens Euthyphro’s statements to the works of Daedalus, whom he calls his ancestor.[^daedalus] When Euthyphro turns the jest back on him, Socrates answers that apparently he is a more clever artist than Daedalus, since he seems to move the works of others as well as his own,[^clever] and that he would rather have his words stay fixed than have the wisdom of Daedalus and the wealth of Tantalus.[^tantalus] Then he offers to help.[^aid] He asks whether everything holy is right,[^holyright] and Euthyphro says it is;[^ido] then whether everything right is also holy.[^also]

## Two views {#views}

:::views{unresolved}
- socrates: The statements are Euthyphro’s, not his.[^yours]
- euthyphro: It is not he but Socrates who makes the statements move; left to Euthyphro, they would have stayed.[^you]
:::
