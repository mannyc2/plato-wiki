# Euthyphro

A man who speaks in the assembly about divine things and foretells the future,[^foretell] with a case of his own before the king:[^action] he is prosecuting his own father for murder.[^murder] He claims exact knowledge of such things,[^exact] and when Socrates presses him to begin again, he leaves in a hurry.[^hurry]

## Where we find him {#scene}

:::passage{ref="euthyphro 4a" from="Such a man" to="Murder, Socrates." lit="My father."}
His case comes out one short answer at a time.
:::

:::passage{ref="euthyphro 15e" from="Some other time" to="time for me to go."}
He leaves with Socrates’ question still open.
:::

## How he argues {#moves}

:::moves
1. In a killing, the only question is whether it was justified,[^justified] and if not, one must proceed against the killer even if he shares one’s hearth.[^hearth]
2. Holiness is doing what he is doing now: prosecuting the wrongdoer.[^now]
3. Men believe that Zeus, the best and most just of the gods, put his own father in bonds,[^zeus] yet they are angry with Euthyphro for proceeding against his; so they are inconsistent.[^inconsistent]
4. What is dear to the gods is holy.[^dear]
5. What all the gods love is holy, and what they all hate is unholy.[^all]
6. Holiness is the part of the right that has to do with attention to the gods.[^attention]
7. Holiness is knowing how to say and do what is gratifying to the gods, in praying and sacrificing.[^gratifying]
:::
