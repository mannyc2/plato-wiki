## What’s happening {#happening}

If Euthyphro accepts these tales, Socrates says, he too must give way, since he confesses he knows nothing about them.[^nothing] Euthyphro says he believes them, and still more wonderful things that most people do not know.[^wonderful] Asked whether he believes there was really war among the gods, with fearful enmities and battles,[^war] he says not only these things, and offers to tell many more;[^more] but Socrates puts them off to another time and returns to his question.[^leisure] Euthyphro’s first answer, Socrates says, only told him that what Euthyphro is doing now is holy.[^doing] Euthyphro holds that it was true,[^true] but grants that many other things are holy too.[^many] Socrates reminds him that the question was not for one or two of the many holy acts, but for the aspect by which all holy acts are holy,[^aspect] and asks for that aspect, to use as a model for judging any act.[^model] Euthyphro’s second answer: what is dear to the gods is holy.[^dear]
