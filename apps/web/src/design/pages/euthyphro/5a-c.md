## What’s happening {#happening}

Euthyphro answers that he would be of no use, and no different from other men, if he did not have exact knowledge of all such things.[^exact] Then the best thing for him, Socrates says, is to become Euthyphro’s pupil before Meletus’ suit comes on.[^pupil] He imagines what he would say to Meletus: if Meletus grants that Euthyphro is wise in such matters, he should believe that Socrates holds correct opinions too;[^correct] if not, he should bring his suit against the teacher instead, for corrupting the old.[^teacher] Euthyphro fancies that if Meletus tried to indict him, he would find Meletus’ weak spot.[^weak] Socrates says Meletus does not seem to notice Euthyphro at all,[^notice] yet has seen through Socrates so easily that he has indicted him for impiety.[^impiety] So he asks Euthyphro to say what he claimed to know so well: what piety and impiety are.[^nature]

[^teacher]: euthyphro 5b "bring a suit against him, my teacher, rather than against me, and charge him with corrupting the old"
[^weak]: euthyphro 5c "I fancy I should find his weak spot"
[^notice]: euthyphro 5c "neither this fellow Meletus, nor anyone else, seems to notice you at all"

[^nature]: euthyphro 5c "What do you say is the nature of piety and impiety"
