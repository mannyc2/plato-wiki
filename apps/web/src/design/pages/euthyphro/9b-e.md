## What’s happening {#happening}

If Euthyphro can show this, Socrates promises, he will glorify Euthyphro’s wisdom as long as he lives.[^glorify] Euthyphro says it is perhaps no small task, though he could show it quite clearly.[^task] When Socrates suggests that Euthyphro thinks him slower than the judges, to whom he will plainly show that such acts are wrong,[^judges] Euthyphro agrees he will show them quite clearly, if they listen to him.[^listen] But Socrates says he asked himself what such a proof would teach him about what holiness is,[^learned] since they had seen that what is hateful to the gods is also dear to them.[^hateful] So he lets Euthyphro off the point;[^letoff] if Euthyphro likes, all the gods may think the act wrong and hate it.[^may] Instead he proposes a correction: whatever all the gods hate is unholy, whatever they all love is holy, and what some love and others hate is neither or both.[^emend] Euthyphro takes it up as his answer: what all the gods love is holy, and what they all hate is unholy.[^all] Asked whether they should accept a statement merely because someone makes it, or inquire into it, Euthyphro says they ought to inquire, though he thinks it correct now.[^inquire]
