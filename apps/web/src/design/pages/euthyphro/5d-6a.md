## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates first asks whether holiness is not always the same with itself in every action,[^same] and whether whatever is unholy does not have some one characteristic quality.[^one] Euthyphro agrees.[^certainly] His first answer is that holiness is doing what he is doing now: prosecuting the wrongdoer who commits murder or steals from the temples or does any such thing.[^now] Whether the wrongdoer is one’s father, one’s mother or anyone else, he says, not prosecuting him is unholy.[^anyone] He offers what he calls a sure proof.[^proof] Those who are incensed with him for proceeding against his father, he says, are inconsistent in what they say about the gods and about him.[^inconsistent]

## Two views {#views}

:::views{unresolved}
- euthyphro: What men believe of the gods supports him: Zeus, whom they hold the best and most just of the gods, put his own father in bonds for wickedly devouring his children.[^zeus]
- socrates: He finds such stories about the gods hard to accept, and asks whether that is why he is being prosecuted.[^hard]
:::
