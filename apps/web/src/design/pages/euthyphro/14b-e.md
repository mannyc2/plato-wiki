## What’s happening {#happening}

Euthyphro says it is a long task to learn all this accurately,[^long] but puts it simply: to know how to say and do what is gratifying to the gods, in praying and sacrificing, is holiness,[^gratifying] and such things keep families and states safe.[^salvation] Socrates says Euthyphro could, if he wished, have answered far more briefly,[^briefly] and that he turned aside just when he was close to the answer;[^aside] but, Socrates says, he must follow wherever Euthyphro leads.[^follow] Step by step, Euthyphro accepts Socrates’ restatement.

:::moves
1. Holiness is a kind of science of sacrificing and praying.[^science]
2. Sacrificing is making gifts to the gods, and praying is asking from them.[^gifts]
3. So holiness would be a science of giving and asking.[^giving]
4. The right way of asking is to ask them for what we need from them,[^need] and the right way of giving is to give them what they need from us,[^present] for it would not be scientific giving to give anyone what he does not need.[^scientific]
5. So holiness would be an art of barter between gods and men.[^barter]
:::

Euthyphro accepts the word “barter,” if Socrates likes to call it so.[^like] Socrates does not like to call it so if it is not true,[^nottrue] and asks what advantage the gods get from the gifts they receive from us.[^advantage]
