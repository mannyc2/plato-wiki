## What’s happening {#happening}

If the Athenians are in earnest, Socrates says, only soothsayers like Euthyphro can tell how it will end.[^soothsayers] Asked about his own case, Euthyphro says he is not defending but prosecuting,[^prosecuting] and that people think him insane for it.[^insane] The man he prosecutes is his own father,[^father] and the charge is murder.[^murder] Socrates fancies that not everyone could do this, only one already far advanced in wisdom;[^wisdom] Euthyphro agrees that he is very far advanced.[^far]

## Two views {#views}

:::views{unresolved}
- socrates: Euthyphro would surely not charge his own father with murder on a stranger’s account.[^stranger]
- euthyphro: It does not matter whether the dead man was a stranger or a relative; the only question is whether the killing was justified.[^justified]
:::
