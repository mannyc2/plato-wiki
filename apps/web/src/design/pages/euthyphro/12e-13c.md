## What’s happening {#happening}

Socrates asks to be taught what part of the right holiness is, so that he can tell Meletus to stop prosecuting him for impiety.[^meletus] Euthyphro’s answer: the part of the right that has to do with attention to the gods is piety and holiness,[^attention] and the remaining part of the right has to do with men.[^men] Socrates thinks this correct,[^correct] but does not yet understand what Euthyphro means by attention.[^mean] Euthyphro agrees to each step but the last.

:::moves
1. Horsemanship is the art of attending to horses,[^horses] and the oxherd’s art is that of attending to oxen.[^oxen]
2. So holiness and piety are the art of attending to the gods.[^art]
3. Attention aims at some good or benefit to what is attended to, as horses are benefited and made better by the horseman’s art.[^benefit]
4. Care and attention are never meant to injure what is cared for.[^injury]
5. Then holiness, as the art of attending to the gods, would benefit the gods and make them better.[^better]
:::

Asked whether a holy act makes one of the gods better,[^holyact] Euthyphro answers, “No, by Zeus, not I.”[^notI] Socrates says he never thought Euthyphro meant that.[^nor]
